## §1 — Two different things, currently conflated

⚠⚠ **The stage says where the work has got to. The review status says whether anyone outside has
looked at it. They are orthogonal and both belong on a document.**

**"First Scrutiny" is not a stage.** It is a **review status**, and under Charlie's definition it is
true of everything produced in stages 1, 2 and 3 — a briefing, a kernel and a deepened proposal
alike. **That is why it cannot also be the name of stage 2.**

⚠ **CC has already applied "First Scrutiny" to the two new documents as though it were a stage.
Correct that.** The label is not wrong on them — it is simply describing their review status, which
is what it should have been doing all along.

## §2 — The seven stages, as product vocabulary

| | |
|---|---|
| **1 · The First Pass** | what is already there |
| **2 · The First Draft** | what you propose, specifically |
| **3 · The Deepening** | what it would actually take |
| **4 · Private Scrutiny** | what your allies say is wrong |
| **5 · Public Scrutiny** | what your opponents say is wrong |
| **6 · Submission** | into the system that decides |
| **7 · In Force** | did it work |

**2a.** ⚠ **Stage 2 is "The First Draft", not "First Scrutiny".** Change it everywhere.
**2b.** ⚠ **Stage 6 is "Submission", not "Parliamentary submission"** — a select committee inquiry, a
consultation, a department and a private member's bill are all doors.
**2c.** ⚠ **Report where the product's existing three stages — The Idea, The Strategy, The
Deepening — sit against these seven.** They are not the same scheme and **two competing stage models
in one product is worse than either.** Report before changing the workspace's own stage header.

## §3 — Decision 102: In Force is stated, not built

⚠ **Charlie: "state it, we can't build it yet, we have nothing to build it with."**

**3a.** It appears in the stage list and in the FAQ **from day one** — it tells a reader what the
platform is for.
**3b.** ⚠ **It is marked as not yet available, in the same four-state form used everywhere else.**
**3c.** ⚠ **Build nothing for it.** It needs post-implementation review tracking, which sits with
Search and does not exist.

## §4 — Decision 101: the stage banner, on every document

**At the top of every generated document, before anything else:**
1. ⚠ **The stage, in bold** — *This is a Stage 2 document: The First Draft.*
2. **The review status** — see §5.
3. **The seven stages, one line each, with this one marked.**
4. **The short caveat**, verbatim from `SEVEN_STAGES.md`:
> **Scrutiny requires something to scrutinise.** A proposal that says *"we would change this by
> legislation"* cannot be argued with. One that says *"we would repeal section 12 and replace it with
> this text"* can — and being argued with is the point. **A specific proposal may be specifically
> wrong, and that is how it improves.** Vagueness is not caution; it is the absence of a proposal.

**4b.** ⚠ **Every document, internal ones included.** Charlie's decision. **Internal documents get
forwarded too, and a reader must never have to guess how finished a document is.**

## §5 — The review status, and it needs a counterpart

⚠⚠ **A flag that only ever disappears teaches nobody anything.** If "First Scrutiny" means *nobody
outside has looked*, then its removal must mean *somebody has* — and the document must say which.

**At minimum three states:**
- **First Scrutiny** — no external review.
- **Privately reviewed** — through stage 4, ⚠ **with the number of reviewers and the date.**
- **Publicly reviewed** — through stage 5, likewise.

**5a.** ⚠ **Report whether the platform can tell.** Does anything record that a review happened?
**If nothing does, say so** — then the status stays at First Scrutiny for everything, honestly, and
the counterparts wait on the review system from §24 of the original design.
**5b.** ⚠ **Do not infer a review from a contribution or a comment.** A review is a deliberate act,
and guessing at one is the kind of claim this platform exists not to make.
