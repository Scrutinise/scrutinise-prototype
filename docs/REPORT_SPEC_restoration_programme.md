# REPORT SPEC — The Restoration Programme: a scrutiny report

**Working title:** *Repeal and Restoration: what the programme would require*
**Companion:** a short summary paper, written last, from this document's finished numbers.
**Audience:** the proposers and whichever lawyer they hand it to.

---

## 1. What this document is

It answers one question, measure by measure: **if you did this, what would you actually have to do?**

It does not argue for the programme or against it. It sets out what breaks, gives the options where
options exist, counts what the job consists of, and hands back the questions only the proposer can
answer. Where the evidence supports the proposer's own claim, it says so; where it doesn't, it says
that too, in the same voice.

Three rules govern everything in it:

1. **Every assertion resolves to a corpus row** — a section number, a neutral citation, a Hansard
   column, a named report with a page. No exceptions, including for statements that seem obvious.
2. **Every number states its denominator** — what it is a count of, and what it is a fraction of.
3. **Gaps are declared, never omitted.** A stated limitation is worth more than a silent one, and a
   reader who finds an undeclared gap discounts everything else in the document.

---

## 2. Front matter

**Preface — what this is and is not.** One page. States the three rules above, states that the
document takes no position on whether the programme should be attempted, and states the line: *we
solve drafting problems, we do not resolve political choices.*

**How to read it.** The document is long because the evidence is long. Names which parts are prose,
which are tables, and which are reference material nobody should read straight through.

**What we could not see.** The coverage statement, generated from live state, in plain words. Case
law from 2001 only. Treaty layer not built. The 37% pre-1963 extraction residual with its measured
effect. Unresolved name-span count. This goes at the *front*, not in an appendix — a reader who meets
the limitations after 60 pages feels misled; one who meets them first trusts the rest.

**Method.** How references were found, how they were verified, what the verification rate was, and
what a reader would have to do to check any single claim themselves.

**Status.** This is a first pass. Its purpose is to show the shape and depth of what the programme
entails, not to complete it. Three measures of twelve are worked; the rest are scoped. Carrying the
programme through would require planning, strategy and resourcing beyond anything in this document,
and the document says so rather than implying otherwise by its length.

**Quotation discipline.** The proposer's words are drawn from transcripts, one of which is
machine-generated. ⚠ **Every quoted sentence attributed to a named person must be verified against
the source recording at its timestamp before print.** An auto-transcript mis-hears names, statutes
and technical terms, and a misquotation in a document handed to the person quoted destroys the
document. Each quote carries its source and locator; unverifiable passages are paraphrased and
labelled as such, never quoted.

## 3. Part 1 — The thesis, stated as a strategy kernel

The proposer's argument set out in the platform's standard form — diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent
actions — sourced to his own words, quoted and located.

**Purpose is verification, not summary.** Stating someone's position back to them in disciplined form
is the fastest way to surface where we have misread them, and any misreading found here invalidates
everything downstream. Every point of interpretation is flagged as such: *"we have read this as X; if
you meant Y, sections 4.2 and 6.1 change."*

Includes the **interlock claim** stated as a testable proposition — the proposer's position is that
the measures must move together because sequential change is defeated by the remainder. Part 2 tests
it.

---

## 4. Part 2 — Programme-level findings

The cross-cutting material that belongs to no single measure. This is the intellectual core and the
part most likely to be read closely.

**4.1 Pattern collapse.** N references reduce to M distinct drafting patterns. The finding that
reframes the programme from impossible to large: the drafting burden is proportional to distinct
patterns, not to reference count. Stated with the method and the counts for all three worked
measures.

**4.2 The absorption problem and the annulment device.** The most important section in the document.

The proposer's own claim is that repeal is insufficient because the courts have absorbed Convention
principles into the common law, and that annulment — declaring the provisions never to have had
effect — is required instead. Test it in three parts:

- **Is the absorption claim correct?** Verify against the authorities on whether the common law
  protects the relevant rights independently of the statute. Report the answer whichever way it falls.
- **Would annulment achieve what he wants?** Set out what the device can and cannot reach — including
  the point that deeming a statute never to have had effect does not unmake the reasoning of cases
  decided under it. A judgment's ratio survives the statute that occasioned it.
- **What have similar devices actually achieved?** Ouster and notwithstanding clauses have a
  litigation record. Set out the drafting approaches with what each has been held to do.

**4.3 The historical analogy, tested.** The proposer invokes Henry VIII's annulments as the model.
Examine what the relevant statutes mechanically did, and whether that machinery has a modern
analogue. This engages him on his own ground — he argues from precedent, and testing his precedent
rigorously is the most respectful thing the document can do. Report what it shows either way.

**4.4 The devolution wall.** ECHR compliance is written into the devolution statutes themselves.
Which measures require amending them, what Sewel engages, and what follows from consent being
refused.

**4.5 Northern Ireland.** Three published expert positions set out fairly with their authorities —
Prosperity Institute, Policy Exchange, CAJ — and what each would need to be true. **Do not
adjudicate.** State what turns on which is right.

**4.6 Instrument allocation.** What can be done by Act, by statutory instrument, by prerogative, by
resolution of both Houses — with the reasons. Where the proposer's stated route is unavailable, say
so and give the available one.

**4.7 The interlock graph.** Dependencies between measures, typed: requires, blocks, duplicates,
weakens. Whether the proposer's "all at once" claim is supported by the structure. This is the
section that either vindicates the thesis's central strategic assertion or doesn't.

## 5. Part 3 — Three worked measures

CRAG 2010 Part 1 · Human Rights Act 1998 · Equality Act 2010.

**Identical chapter structure for all three, without variation.** The repetition is the point: it
demonstrates an instrument rather than an essay, and it is what makes "the same method applies to the
other nine" credible rather than asserted.

| § | Section | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The measure in the proposer's own words | Transcripts, quoted and located |
| 2 | What the target law currently does | Corpus |
| 3 | Inbound reference map — pattern table, counts, coverage block | `inbound()` |
| 4 | Dispositions per pattern, with a verbatim worked example | Classification pass |
| 5 | Devolution gate | Corpus |
| 6 | International gate | Corpus, treaty texts |
| 7 | Northern Ireland gate | Corpus, published positions |
| 8 | Absorption gate — does the principle survive repeal? | Case law |
| 9 | Historic objections to this *shape* of measure | Argument graph |
| 10 | Counterparty response, where a treaty is engaged | Treaty clauses |
| 11 | Second-order proposals this measure spawns | Analysis |
| 12 | Open questions for the proposer | Analysis |
| 13 | What we could not establish, and why | Gaps register |

**Rule for §4:** every pattern carries one verbatim example of the actual statutory words. A quoted
provision does more work than a paragraph explaining it. The single most persuasive line in the CRAG
pilot is a fragment of statute, not a summary of one.

**Rule for §9:** the argument graph returns objections made in Parliament, often about entirely
unrelated subjects. Each is quoted with speaker, date and debate. These are objections somebody
actually made, not objections a model generated, and the distinction should be visible on the page.

**Rule for §12:** open questions are questions, not disguised recommendations. If there is a
determinate technical answer, it belongs in §4 as a disposition. If the choice is political, it stays
a question.

## 7. Part 5 — What the work consists of

The **composition** of the job, not its cost.

⚠ **This document puts no time or money figure on the work, and says why.** We have no hours model
and no rate structure, so any such figure would be invented — and an invented number in a document
whose first rule is that every assertion resolves to a source would discredit everything around it.
What we can count, we count.

**7.1 What is countable, and is counted:**

- Distinct drafting patterns per measure, and in total across the three worked measures
- Discrete decisions the proposer must take before drafting can begin
- Separate legislative instruments required — Acts, statutory instruments, resolutions, treaty acts
- Gates engaged per measure, and which are unresolved
- Second-order proposals spawned, each a piece of work in its own right
- Questions this analysis could not answer, by category

**7.2 Composition of expertise required.** What kinds of people the work needs, not how many hours of
each:

| Tier | Requires | Task |
|---|---|---|
| A | No legal training | Verify a reference resolves correctly against the source |
| B | Law student, paralegal, retired solicitor | Classify a reference into a disposition from a fixed taxonomy |
| C | Qualified lawyer | Adjudicate contested dispositions; the absorption questions |
| D | Parliamentary counsel | Drafting |

The useful observation is that the work decomposes at all — a large share sits at tiers A and B and
does not require scarce expertise. That is a structural fact about the job and it follows from the
pattern-collapse finding. It is stated as an observation, not as a recruitment proposition.

**7.3 What this pass did not reach.** The nine unworked measures, the case law layer, the treaty
layer, and the second-order proposals. Named so the reader can see the boundary of what has been
established.

## 7A. Closing note — what this instrument is

One short section, factual, no pitch. What Scrutinise is: a free, non-partisan platform that takes a
proposal and works out what delivering it would actually require. It is available on identical terms
to anyone, and the same instrument is available for the remaining nine measures, for the second-order
proposals this analysis has spawned, and for organising and coordinating the people who would do the
work.

Written in the same register as the rest of the document. If it reads as a sales page it has failed;
the analysis is the argument.

## 9. Length and shape

Expect 60–100 pages, of which the majority is tables and quoted statutory text. **Prose stays tight.**
The document is long because the evidence is long — not because the argument is. Any section where
prose exceeds the evidence it carries is over-written and gets cut.

---

## 10. What this document must never do

- Recommend whether the programme should be attempted.
- Assert a consequence the graph did not return.
- Present a count as complete.
- Compress 1,800 references into a confident paragraph. Group and count; never summarise away.
- Let a model-generated objection sit alongside a sourced one without the difference being visible.
