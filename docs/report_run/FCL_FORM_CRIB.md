## STEP 5 — PUBLIC STATEMENT

**15. Public statement** (148 words)

```
Scrutinise is a free, not-for-profit platform that helps members of the public, campaigners and parliamentarians understand legislation and develop well-evidenced proposals for changing it. Its purpose is better public engagement with law-making.

We hold UK legislation, parliamentary debates, committee reports and regulatory guidance in one searchable corpus. Judgments from Find Case Law let us show how the courts have interpreted a statutory provision, so that someone asking "what duty does a council owe a person who is homeless?" is shown the section, the debate behind it, and the cases that construe it.

Our methods are full-text and semantic search over published judgment text, together with citation extraction linking judgments to the provisions they interpret. We do not generate automated case summaries, rank judges or parties, or give legal advice. Every result links to the judgment on Find Case Law as the authoritative version.
```

## STEP 7 — THE NINE PRINCIPLES (Q29)

If it is one box, paste the whole block. If it is nine boxes, paste each numbered paragraph into its own.

**Opening paragraph — paste first if there is a single box:**
```
Scrutinise is a free, not-for-profit platform for public understanding of legislation. We use Find Case Law to show how courts have construed a statutory provision. We publish no automated case summaries, no predictions, and no legal advice. Where we identify a defect in our own handling of the records, we measure it and say so; several of the risks below are ones we have already found and are fixing.
```

**1. Dignity of the court**
```
Risk: presenting judicial reasoning in a way that trivialises or distorts it. We quote judgment text as published and link to the judgment on Find Case Law as the authoritative version. We do not paraphrase a holding into a headline, generate summaries, or editorialise on the merits of a decision. Judgment text is displayed with its neutral citation and court, so a reader always knows which court decided what. We do not permit user comment or annotation on judgment text.
```

**2. Independence of the court**
```
Risk: material that appears to lobby the judiciary or to characterise particular judges. We do not analyse or publish anything about individual judges — no rankings, no outcome rates by judge, no profiles. The platform's advocacy function is directed at Parliament and legislative change, and it is architecturally separate from the case-law search. We do not predict outcomes, and we do not offer any tool for assessing how a particular judge or court is likely to decide.
```

**3. Appropriate scrutiny**
```
Risk: users treating our corpus as complete, and jigsaw identification. We treat incompleteness as a measured property, not a caveat. Find Case Law's coverage begins in 2001 for the Court of Appeal (Civil Division) and 2003 for the Criminal Division and the High Court divisions, so we hold effectively no English case law before 2003. We have tested this: of ten leading pre-2003 authorities, none was returned and three returned a different case with a similar name. We are building an explicit coverage-boundary declaration so that the platform states the era it can and cannot reach, rather than answering from the nearest match. We will not put Find Case Law material in front of users under a licensed use until that declaration is live. On jigsaw identification, we do not aggregate personal attributes across records, build person-level profiles, or link judgment parties to any external dataset; we index judgments as discrete documents.
```

**4. Anti-discriminatory harm**
```
Risk: semantic search surfacing records in a way that correlates with protected characteristics, or a wrong record being presented as the right one. We monitor for the failure we have actually observed — a confident wrong answer — because that is the mechanism by which harm reaches a person named in a record. We hold a fixed evaluation set of questions and check retrieval against it, and we require an explicit "not held" response in preference to a nearest match. Users can report a result; a reported result is withdrawn from retrieval while it is investigated, and the investigation records the cause and the fix. We do not filter, rank or weight records by any protected characteristic, and no such attribute is extracted or stored. This commitment describes how our retrieval behaves; it is not a restriction on the subject matter of a proposal. A user is free to develop a proposal addressing a perceived disparity, including one concerning a protected characteristic, and the statistical evidence for such a proposal is drawn from published sources rather than from judgment records. We place no restriction on what a user may propose — whether a proposal deserves to progress is a judgement for Parliament, not for us.
```

**5. Anti-bias**
```
Risk: drift over time as the corpus and the retrieval model change. Every ingest and retrieval change is recorded in a dated change log with a prediction written before the measurement and scored after it, so a change that degrades results is visible rather than absorbed. Corpus composition is tracked per collection against the publisher's own enumeration, so a shift in what we hold is measurable rather than assumed. We have found and corrected several defects this way, including embedded text that was formatting code rather than judgment text. Retrieval quality is measured against a fixed question set, not against impressions.
```

**6. Personal privacy**
```
Risk: handling personal data in the records without an adequate basis or adequate security. We understand that this licence is neither a data-sharing nor a processing agreement, and that establishing a lawful basis for processing personal data in the records is our responsibility. We process only judgment text as published by Find Case Law; we do not enrich it with any external personal data, and we do not create person-level records. Data is held in access-controlled UK and EU cloud infrastructure with authentication on all administrative access. We honour take-downs: where a record is revised or withdrawn by the court, it is removed from our corpus and our search index (see Principle 9). We will act on any objection or erasure request in respect of material we hold.
```

**7. Discoverability** — ⚠ **YOUR CALL: this is the paragraph you have not signed off. Read it before pasting.**
```
Risk: our pages making judgment content search-engine discoverable, which the Ministry of Justice and The National Archives have deliberately chosen not to do. We accept this as a term. Pages that display judgment text carry noindex, nofollow, and our robots.txt disallows those paths for all crawlers, including AI crawlers, which we enumerate and maintain. We do not publish sitemaps covering judgment content. Judgment text is not exposed through any public API or bulk export, and we do not offer a download of Find Case Law material. Rate limiting is implemented and is applied today to our AI and data-export endpoints; extending it to the retrieval surfaces, and adding monitoring for automated collection, are committed before licensed use begins. We will audit our own site for search-engine visibility of judgment content before licensed use begins and at least annually after.
```

**8. Algorithmic transparency**
```
Risk: users not knowing how a result was produced. Our retrieval method is published in plain English on the site: keyword and semantic search over published text, with the ranking signals named. Where a generative model is used to phrase an answer, that is disclosed on the response, and the underlying source is cited and linked so a user can check the original rather than take our word for it. Our methodology documentation is public and we do not treat retrieval behaviour as a trade secret.
```

**9. Accurate data representation**
```
Risk: over-claiming the significance of what we hold, and serving a record the court has since revised or withdrawn. Coverage is stated per collection with the date range and the known gaps, and we distinguish between "we do not hold this" and "this does not exist" — the difference matters and we treat conflating them as a defect. On revisions and take-downs we should be precise about what exists today: handling is currently manual, and no scheduled job re-checks Find Case Law for revised or withdrawn records. We will have an automated scheduled re-check in place before any licensed use begins, removing or replacing affected records in both the database and the search index. The design is already settled by experience rather than by intention: we have found that correcting a database record does not by itself change what a user sees, so the process requires the index refresh to be verified rather than assumed. Every result names its court, citation and date, and links to Find Case Law as the authoritative version.
```
