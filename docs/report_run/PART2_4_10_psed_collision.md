# Part 2 §4.10 — Worked example: what repealing the public sector equality duty actually touches

**Draft 1, 2 Sep 2026, CCW.** Built from the Scrutinise collision index of the statute book,
generated 28 August 2026. Every number below states what it counts and out of what. **None is
presented as complete.**

---

## Why this one, and why now

Until a fortnight ago this would have been a hypothetical. It is not.

**Public Sector Equality Duty (Repeal) Bill, Bill 4189**, sponsored by **Joy Morrissey MP**, was
given a first reading on **22 June 2026**. Its long title is:

> "A Bill to repeal section 149 and schedule 18 of the Equality Act 2010; to make provision
> consequential on that repeal; and for connected purposes."

⚠⚠ **The Bill has no published text.** Parliament's own page records: *"There are no publications for
this Bill yet."* The same is true of the **European Convention on Human Rights (Notification of
Withdrawal) Bill, Bill 4242**.

**So both live Bills state the intention and not the mechanism.** The words "provision consequential
on that repeal" are a placeholder for work nobody has yet published. **That is the gap this section
fills**, and it is the clearest illustration in the report of what column two of the register is for:
*if you say you want this, here is what would have to change.*

---

## What the section does not do on its own

Section 149 is not free-standing. **Chapter 1 of Part 11 of the Equality Act 2010 is a nine-section
machine built to operate one duty**, and the duty is s.149:

| Provision | Side-heading | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **s.149** | Public sector equality duty | **The duty itself** |
| s.150 | Public authorities and public functions | Says who the duty binds — **introduces Schedule 19** |
| s.151 | Power to specify public authorities | Power to **amend Schedule 19** |
| s.152 | Power to specify public authorities: consultation | Procedure for s.151 |
| s.153 | Power to impose specific duties | Power to make regulations imposing **specific duties** to secure better performance of s.149 |
| s.154 | Power to impose specific duties: cross-border authorities | s.153 for bodies straddling the border |
| s.155 | Power to impose specific duties: supplementary | Machinery for s.153 |
| s.156 | Enforcement | How s.149 is enforced |
| s.157 | Interpretation | Definitions for the Chapter |
| **Schedule 18** | *Public sector equality duty: exceptions* | Introduced by **s.149**. Carves out children, immigration, judicial functions |
| **Schedule 19** | *Public authorities* | Introduced by **s.150**. The list of bodies the duty binds |

⚠⚠ **The Bill as titled repeals two of these eleven: s.149 and Schedule 18.**

**The remaining nine exist only to service the duty.** Sections 150 to 157 and Schedule 19 would
survive as machinery for a duty that no longer exists — a power to specify public authorities for
nothing, a power to impose specific duties in support of nothing, an enforcement section with nothing
to enforce, and a list of several hundred named public bodies in Schedule 19 pointing at a repealed
section.

**This is not a criticism of the Bill.** Its long title expressly reserves consequential provision,
and a presentation Bill without a printed text is a statement of intent. **It is a statement of the
size of the thing that has been reserved**, and of what a drafter would have to work through.

## What else in the statute book points at it

**What was searched.** The Scrutinise collision index, run against the Equality Act 2010 as a whole
on 28 August 2026. Three detection types, **reported separately and never added together** because
they are different strengths of evidence:

- **`markup`** — the citing document names the Act by URI in its own markup. The strongest: the
  source asserts the target by identity.
- **`text`** — the Act's name resolved in running text. **The target is derived, not read from the
  document**, so a text edge is never quoted as the source's own words.
- **`enabling`** — the instrument's own enacting words say it was **made under** the target. ⚠ **A
  different and stronger fact:** an instrument that merely mentions an Act survives the Act's repeal;
  **an instrument whose enabling power is repealed may fall with it.**

**The denominator.** The Equality Act file holds **2,016 rows**. Of those, **1,256 resolve to the Act
but to no particular provision** — they name the Act, not a section. **Those 1,256 can be neither
counted into the figures below nor ruled out of them.** Everything that follows is drawn from the
760 rows that do resolve to a provision.

### Rows in the file that point at the public-sector-duty cluster: **162**

| Target | Rows | Detection |
|---|---|---|
| **Schedule 19** (the list of public authorities) | **103** | all `text` |
| **s.149** (the duty) | **40** | 39 `text`, 1 `markup` |
| **s.151** (power to specify public authorities) | **12** | all `enabling` |
| **s.153** (power to impose specific duties) | **6** | all `enabling` |
| **s.155** (specific duties: supplementary) | **1** | `enabling` |
| Schedule 18, ss.150, 152, 154, 156, 157 | **0** | — |

⚠ **Schedule 19 is cited two and a half times more often than the duty itself.** That is what you
would expect of a list of named bodies, and it is why leaving Schedule 19 standing after repealing
s.149 is not a tidy-up point: it is the most heavily cross-referenced part of the cluster.

## ⚠⚠ The finding: this is a devolution measure

**Where the 162 citing provisions come from:**

| Jurisdiction of the citing instrument | Rows | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Scotland — Scottish statutory instruments | 51 | 31% |
| **UK Acts of Parliament** | 50 | 31% |
| Wales — Welsh statutory instruments | 26 | 16% |
| UK-wide statutory instruments | 25 | 15% |
| Scotland — Acts of the Scottish Parliament | 6 | 4% |
| Wales — Acts/Measures of the Senedd | 4 | 2% |
| **Northern Ireland** | **0** | **0%** |

**87 of the 162 — 54% — are devolved instruments.**

### And on the strongest evidence, the imbalance is near-total

**Nineteen instruments were made under the PSED regulation-making powers** — that is, their enabling
authority is s.151, s.153 or s.155 of the Equality Act 2010. These are the instruments that do not
merely mention the duty; **they exist because of it, and repealing the power puts them in question.**

| Made under | Instruments | Where from |
|---|---|---|
| **s.151** — power to specify public authorities | 12 | 9 Scottish, 2 Welsh, 1 UK-wide |
| **s.153** — power to impose specific duties | 6 | 5 Scottish, 1 Welsh |
| **s.155** — specific duties: supplementary | 1 | 1 Scottish |

> ⚠⚠ **Eighteen of the nineteen are Scottish or Welsh. One is UK-wide. None is
> English-only, and none is Northern Irish.**

**What this means in ordinary words.** The public sector equality duty is a Westminster provision, but
**the working machinery built on top of it is overwhelmingly Scottish and Welsh.** Scotland and Wales
took the power in s.153 and used it, repeatedly, over fifteen years — the Scottish specific duties
regulations of 2011 through to 2025, and the Welsh equivalents. England did not build the same
superstructure.

**So the practical effect of repealing the duty and its powers falls hardest where the programme has
the least authority to act**, and it does so on the strongest class of evidence in our index rather
than the weakest.

⚠ **This is the devolution wall of §4.5, appearing in a place nobody looked for it.** It is not an
argument about Sewel consent in the abstract. It is nineteen named instruments, fifteen of them
Scottish, whose enabling power the Bill would remove.

## ⚠⚠ Northern Ireland: the prediction and the check

§4.9 sets out the statutory position: **s.217(3) of the Equality Act 2010 extends only three
provisions to Northern Ireland — ss.82, 105(3)–(4) and 199 — and s.149 is not among them.**

**That produces a testable prediction: no Northern Ireland instrument should appear anywhere in the
public-sector-duty cluster.**

**The prediction holds. Of 162 rows in the cluster, Northern Ireland instruments account for zero.**

**And the check turned up something more interesting than the confirmation.** Northern Ireland
instruments do cite the Equality Act 2010 elsewhere in the file — three rows — and **all three cite
the same provision: s.6, the definition of disability.**

⚠ **That is the shape of the relationship, demonstrated rather than asserted.** Northern Ireland law
sits outside the Equality Act's *duties* while borrowing its *definitions*. A repeal Bill drafted
without noticing this would leave the Northern Ireland duty untouched — and, if it went further and
repealed the whole Act, would silently pull the definition of disability out from under Northern
Ireland instruments that rely on it.

**Neither consequence is on the twelve-point list.**

## What this section establishes

1. **The Bill now before the Commons repeals two of the eleven provisions in the cluster**, and
   reserves the rest to unpublished consequential provision.
2. **Nine provisions and one Schedule would be left as machinery for a duty that no longer exists**,
   including the most heavily cross-referenced item in the cluster.
3. **Nineteen instruments were made under the powers**, and **eighteen of them are devolved.**
4. **Northern Ireland is untouched by the duty and touched by the definitions** — the opposite way
   round from how the measure is described.
5. **All of this is derivable from the statute book in an afternoon, and none of it appears in either
   live Bill.**

⚠ **Every figure here is a count of what we hold.** The index is a snapshot of 28 August 2026, 1,256
of 2,016 rows in the file resolve to the Act without resolving to a provision and are outside these
counts entirely, and the three detection types are not summed. **A drafter would need to repeat this
against the live statute book before relying on it.**

## Open questions

24. **Is the target s.149 alone, or Chapter 1 of Part 11 entire?** The Bill before the Commons takes
    the first. The register reads the thesis as taking the whole Act. *Recommendation: answer this
    before anything else on this measure — everything below it changes with the answer.*
25. **What happens to Schedule 19?** It is introduced by s.150, not s.149, so it survives the Bill as
    titled. It is also the most-cited item in the cluster.
26. **What is the intention towards the eighteen devolved instruments made under ss.151, 153 and
    155?** They can be left, saved, or allowed to fall. Each is a different Bill and a different
    conversation with Edinburgh and Cardiff. *Recommendation: this is the question most likely to
    determine whether the measure is deliverable, and it is currently unasked.*
27. **Is the definition of disability in s.6 intended to survive for Northern Ireland?** On the
    whole-Act reading it would not.
