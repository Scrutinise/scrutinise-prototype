# Part 8 · How to read this Part

## Part 8 · How to read this Part

**This appendix answers one question: who will argue about each measure, and what have they already said?**

It has two halves, and they are built from different material, so they fail in different ways.

- **8.1 · The stakeholder map** — organisations and named individuals with a public platform, drawn from published sources by a research pass and checked by a second one. This is new in the second draft and replaces the thin parliamentary-only register that stood here before.
- **8.2 · The parliamentary record** — divisions and early day motions retrieved from the corpus. It is thin, and the section says so rather than dressing it up.

### The marks, and what they mean here

Every row in 8.1 carries one of three marks. **They are about the evidence, not about the person.**

- **[verified]** — the page was retrieved and the statement read on it.
- **[corroborated, unchecked]** — two or more independent sources report it, but the primary document was not retrieved.
- **[single mention, unchecked]** — one source reports it. A lead to follow, not a citation to rely on.

⚠ **Nothing in this appendix is a finding.** It is a list to work down and confirm. Before any name here is quoted in public, the underlying document should be read.

⚠⚠ **A stance is a stance on *this measure*, not a summary of anyone's politics.** An organisation can be against repealing the Human Rights Act and for curbing the Sentencing Council. Several here are.

**"Likely to engage, position not stated" is not a hedge.** It is the more useful column in planning terms: it names the bodies that will certainly respond to a Bill and have not yet said how. Anyone who could not be pinned to a specific dated statement was put there rather than given a stance.

### M-09 · Gender self-identification

⚠⚠ **The law on this moved before the measure did.** On **16 April 2025** the Supreme Court held unanimously, in *For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers* [2025] UKSC 16, that "sex", "man" and "woman" in the Equality Act 2010 refer to **biological sex**, and that "a person with a GRC in the female gender does not come within the definition of a 'woman' under the EA 2010". Trans people remain protected through the separate characteristic of gender reassignment. [verified] supremecourt.uk press summary, UKSC 2024/0042.

**What follows from that, for this measure:** the part of the objection that concerned the meaning of sex in equality law has been answered by the courts, not by Parliament. What survives is the Gender Recognition Act 2004 itself and the implementation of the ruling. **The measure should be re-stated against the law as it now is, or it will be answered on the floor of the House with a judgment rather than an argument.**

### ⚠⚠ Read this before the tables

⚠ **The eight single names are a gap in the corpus, not a fact about the measures.** The record holds 60,995 early day motions and, for each, only the member who tabled it — against the 2,125,547 signatories Parliament publishes. An early day motion target can therefore only ever return one name.
