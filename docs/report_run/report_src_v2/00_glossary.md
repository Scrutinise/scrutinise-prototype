# Glossary

Terms of art used in this document, defined once here rather than in the body.

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Absorption** | The process by which a right originally created by one statute comes to be protected by others as well, so that repealing the first statute does not remove the right. |
| **Annulment (as used in this programme)** | Legislating to declare that provisions never had legal effect, rather than repealing them prospectively. Distinct from ordinary repeal, and constitutionally far more contested. |
| **Citator** | The tool a lawyer uses to check what has happened to a judgment since it was given — whether later courts have followed it, distinguished it, doubted it or overruled it. |
| **Consequential amendment** | A change to another Act made necessary by the main change, usually to correct cross-references. A repeal of any substantial Act generates many. |
| **Declaration of incompatibility** | A statement by a senior court under section 4 of the Human Rights Act 1998 that a provision cannot be read compatibly with the Convention. It does not strike the provision down; it puts the question back to Parliament. |
| **Devolved / reserved** | A devolved matter is one the Scottish Parliament, Senedd or Northern Ireland Assembly may legislate on; a reserved matter is one Westminster keeps. Whether a measure is devolved determines who has to agree to it. |
| **Enabling power** | A provision in an Act that authorises a minister to make secondary legislation. Provisions reached through an enabling power are counted separately here from those named directly. |
| **Henry VIII power** | A power in an Act allowing a minister to amend or repeal primary legislation by order, without a new Act. Named for the Statute of Proclamations 1539. |
| **Interpretive obligation** | The duty under section 3 of the Human Rights Act 1998 to read legislation compatibly with the Convention so far as it is possible to do so. |
| **Legislative consent motion** | The vote by which a devolved legislature consents to Westminster legislating on a devolved matter. See *Sewel convention*. |
| **Long title** | The opening words of a Bill describing its purpose. It limits what may be added by amendment, so a narrow long title constrains what a Bill can be made to do. |
| **Ouster clause** | A provision seeking to exclude or limit the courts' jurisdiction to review a decision. |
| **Per incuriam** | Of a judgment: reached in ignorance of a binding authority or statutory provision, and therefore not binding on later courts. |
| **Primary / secondary legislation** | Primary legislation is an Act of Parliament. Secondary (or delegated) legislation is made under an Act by a minister, usually as a statutory instrument. |
| **Public sector equality duty** | The duty in section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 requiring public bodies to have due regard to specified equality objectives when exercising their functions. |
| **Remedial order** | Secondary legislation made under section 10 of the Human Rights Act 1998 to correct a provision found incompatible with the Convention. |
| **Sewel convention** | The convention, given statutory recognition but not made enforceable, that Westminster will not normally legislate on devolved matters without the devolved legislature's consent. |
| **Statutory instrument** | The usual form of secondary legislation. Several thousand are made each year. |
| **Sunset clause** | A provision causing an Act or part of it to expire on a stated date unless renewed. |
| **Treatment (of a judgment)** | What a later court did with an earlier decision: followed, applied, distinguished, doubted, disapproved, not followed, read down, per incuriam, or overruled. |
