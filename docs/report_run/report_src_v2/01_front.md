# What is this Document?

**This document takes a political programme and works out what it would take to actually do it.**

That is the whole idea behind Scrutinise. Someone says what they want changed. Scrutinise works through what would have to happen — which laws, which bodies, which decisions, which obstacles — and hands it back with the questions only the proposers can answer.

### Twelve measures, not one

Most of the public discussion treats "the Starkey thesis" as a single idea. It is not. It is at least twelve separate changes, each with its own law, its own opposition and its own difficulty. Some are simple. Some have never been attempted by anyone. Two are already before Parliament in skeleton form.

**Volume 1 looks at the issues relevant to all twelve measures. Volume 2 looks at each measure in isolation, and what each would entail. Volume 3 holds the detailed research sources and references** — including an appendix giving a modern, searchable text of David's 278 YouTube items relating to politics.

### What is in it

**David's Programme**

- The register — the twelve key changes, in David's own words, with the law that would have to change, and why
- Whether repeal alone achieves what is intended, tested against the courts and against two published legal analyses
- Four different plans proposed within a year by people on the same side of the argument, and where they contradict each other
- What repeal does to Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland
- The published positions on each measure, including the ones that cut against David's Programme and the ones that have overtaken it

**For each measure worked in full**

- What the law actually does, and what it does not do
- What else in the law points at it, counted and grouped
- What the courts have said
- What was tried before, and what happened to it
- How hard this will be, and what is most likely to go wrong

## What this is not

**It is not a finished proposal, a recommendation, or a costing. Nothing here argues that this programme should proceed.** It sets out what proceeding would involve.

It is also not complete, and does not pretend to be. It is a first pass whose purpose is to show the shape and depth of what David's Programme entails. Carrying it through would need planning, strategy and resourcing well beyond anything in this document.

**And it is a pilot.** Scrutinise is new, and this is among the first proposals put through it. Where a section misses, saying so is the most useful correction available. Some items will be a simple deletion; others will open something that has to be worked properly.

### Three limits to know first

Every finding traces to a source and is dated. Where the document reasons rather than cites, it says so. Where the record is silent, that is said too — **a gap that names itself is worth more than one that looks like an absence of evidence.**

Three limits you should know before you read anything else.

**One. The record of court judgments used here begins in 2003.** The free public record starts in 2001 at the earliest. Older judgments are available to view, but not to search and analyse. No citator — the tool lawyers use to check whether a judgment has since been doubted or overruled — was available for this pass, so it cannot be confirmed that none of the judgments cited has been doubted or overruled. That check should be done before any of this argument is relied on.

**Two. Quotations from spoken material are machine-transcribed.** Any quotation should be checked against the recording, and the timestamp is given so that it can be.

**Three. Every number says what it counts and out of what.** Where the document says "23 Acts were cited", that means 23 in the material retrieved — not 23 in the world.

This document has not been reviewed by qualified legal opinion. It is the starting point of legislative scrutiny, not the end of it. Expert legal advice is an essential part of the proposer's development of these ideas.

## Marks used in this document

In running text, source confidence is stated in words:

- **[verified]** — the citation has been checked and the document exists
- **[corroborated, unchecked]** — named independently by more than one research pass, but not yet checked against the source
- **[single mention, unchecked]** — named once only, a lead rather than a citation

In tables the same three are marked ✔, ● and ○, with the key printed beneath each table that uses them.
⚠ marks a finding that cuts against the expected answer. ⚠⚠ marks one that changes what a section concludes.
