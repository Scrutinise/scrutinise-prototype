## Where this list came from

**The list is not taken from anyone's summary. It is built from David's own words.**

The source is a corpus of **287 transcripts of David speaking** — the lecture series, the conference addresses, the long-form interviews — held as machine transcripts, searched in full. Where two independent transcription systems agree on a passage, it is treated as sound. Where only one exists, the passage is either checked by ear or turned into a paraphrase and labelled as one. **No quotation in this document rests on a single machine transcript.**

Each of the twelve above is something David is recorded as saying. The register in the next section gives the words.

## ⚠⚠ Why this list is not the list in circulation

A "twelve-point programme" is in general use in the commentary. **It is not the same list.**

**Every item on the circulated list was checked against all 287 transcripts. Four of them cannot be traced to anything David is recorded as saying:**

- Restoring the office of Lord Chancellor
- Repealing the Climate Change Act 2008 or ending the Net Zero duty
- Abolishing the Judicial Appointments Commission
- Making it easier to remove named judges

**And two things David says repeatedly are not on the circulated list at all** — the independence of the Bank of England, and publicly funded charities.

⚠ **The list the programme is being discussed under is not the list David has stated.** That is a fact about the public conversation rather than about David, and correcting it is the first thing this document does. The four unsourced items are printed at the end of the register, marked, so David can confirm, correct or delete them rather than have them silently attributed or silently dropped.

## The assumptions made here, stated so they can be rejected

**Six, and any of them could be wrong.**

**One. Each measure is treated as a separate proposal.** David's own position is that they must move together. They are split so that each can be worked properly, and the "must move together" claim is then tested on its own terms in Part 3.

**Two. Where David describes a complaint rather than a remedy, the complaint is taken as the measure.** The Bank of England is the clearest case: David describes mismanagement since 1998 rather than setting out what should replace independence. It is registered as a measure, with the lower confidence flagged.

**Three. Where David names an example rather than a target, it is worked anyway.** The Sentencing Council is named as an instance of the quango problem, not as a separate item. It is given its own entry so the document can show what dealing with one named body actually costs. **Whether it is one of the twelve is a question for David.**

**Four. Where David names no legal instrument, none is invented for him.** The charities measure is the case: legislation, grant conditions and Charity Commission guidance are all available and produce entirely different results. The options are set out, and the choice is recorded as unmade.

**Five. The complaint is separated from the remedy throughout.** David states almost every measure as a remedy — repeal this, abolish that. **A remedy is not a problem statement**, and working out what has to change requires knowing what is wrong, not only what is to be removed. So each entry states the complaint first and the proposed remedy second. Where the complaint has been got wrong, the entry will be wrong.

**Six. The target is taken to be the law as it stands today**, not as it stood when David described it. On two measures that matters: the courts have moved since, in the direction David wants.

## The three that turned out to be different from expected

Printed here because a reader flipping through should not have to find them.

**Measure 6, the civil service, has an ambiguous target.** David objects to the Northcote-Trevelyan reforms of 1854. The law that exists is the Act of 2010. **Repealing the 2010 Act leaves the 1854 settlement standing as convention** — and the convention, not the statute, is what determines how Whitehall behaves. This is the largest gap in the programme between what is said and what would have to be drafted.

**Measure 9, gender self-identification, may already be delivered.** Self-identification was never enacted in Great Britain, and the Supreme Court decided the point in April 2025 in the direction David wants. What remains is enforcement, not legislation.

**Measures 2, 8 and 9 are substantially one measure**, not three. They converge on the same statute. Part 3 shows the working.
