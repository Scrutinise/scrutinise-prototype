## Where this list came from

**The list is built from David's own words.**

The source is a corpus of **285 recordings of David speaking** — the lecture series, the conference addresses, the long-form interviews — held as machine transcripts, searched in full. ⚠ **All quotations should be checked against the recording before they are relied on.** The timestamp is given in every case so that they can be.

Each of the twelve above is something David is recorded as saying. The register in the next section gives the words.

## ⚠⚠ Why this list is not the list being attributed to David

⚠⚠ **There is no published twelve-point Starkey programme.** A repeal programme is discussed as though there were one, but a search of the public record found no document setting it out. What exists instead is other people's programmes, written by named authors, being read as David's. Three are in circulation:

|---|---|
| **Popular Conservatism, "Conservative Party Embraces David Starkey Thesis", PopCon Newsdesk, 14 May 2026** [verified] | The Conservative Party's Alternative King's Speech, presented under David's name in the headline. ## The assumptions made here, stated so they can be reviewed

**One. Each measure is treated as a separate proposal.** David's own position is that they must move together. They are split so that each can be worked properly, and the "must move together" claim is then tested on its own terms in Part 3.

**Two. Where David describes a complaint rather than a remedy, the complaint is taken as the measure.** The Bank of England is the clearest case: David describes mismanagement since 1998 rather than setting out what should replace independence. It is registered as a measure, with the lower confidence flagged.

**Three. Where David names an example rather than a target, it is worked anyway.** The Sentencing Council is named as an instance of the quango problem, not as a separate item. It is given its own entry so the document can show what dealing with one named body actually costs. **Whether it is one of the twelve is a question for David.**

**Four. Where David names no legal instrument, none is invented for him.** The charities measure is the case: legislation, grant conditions and Charity Commission guidance are all available and produce entirely different results. The options are set out, and the choice is recorded as unmade.

**Five. The complaint is separated from the remedy throughout.** David states almost every measure as a remedy — repeal this, abolish that. **A remedy is not a problem statement**, and working out what has to change starts with identifying the problem, not just the solution. So each entry states the complaint first and the proposed remedy second. Both need to be carefully reviewed.

**Six. The target is taken to be the law as it stands today**, not as it stood when David described it. On two measures that matters: the courts have moved since, in the direction David wants.

## The three that turned out to be different from expected

Printed here because a reader flipping through should not have to find them.

**Measure 6, the civil service, has an ambiguous target.** David objects to the Northcote-Trevelyan reforms of 1854. The law that exists is the Act of 2010. **Repealing the 2010 Act leaves the 1854 settlement standing as convention** — and the convention, not the statute, is what determines how Whitehall behaves. This is the largest gap in David's Programme between what is said and what would have to be drafted.

**Measure 9, gender self-identification, may already be delivered.** Self-identification was never enacted in Great Britain, and the Supreme Court decided the point in April 2025 in the direction David wants. What remains is enforcement, not legislation.

**Measures 2, 8 and 9 are substantially one measure**, not three. They converge on the same statute. Part 3 shows the working.

## Four measures attributed to David that the record does not support

**All 285 recordings were searched, and no statement of any of these four was found.** Each of them does appear in a named published document by somebody else. They are printed here so David can confirm, correct or delete.

| Attributed measure | Where it actually appears | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Restore the office of Lord Chancellor** | Carswell, *Restore the State* (Part 3), 7 Sep 2025 — *"restore appointment powers to the Lord Chancellor"* | Not found in David's words. Note also that the office was never abolished — the Constitutional Reform Act 2005 changed its functions. David may mean restoring the old functions, which is a different and narrower thing |
| **Repeal the Climate Change Act 2008 / end the Net Zero duty** | Conservative Party Alternative King's Speech, reported by Popular Conservatism, 14 May 2026 | Not found in David's words |
| **Abolish the Judicial Appointments Commission** | Carswell, *Restore the State* (Part 3) — *"dissolve the Judicial Appointments Commission (JAC)"* | Not found in David's words |
| **Make it easier to remove named judges** | Carswell, *Restore the State* (Part 3) — *"authority to remove senior judges whose rulings consistently demonstrate personal activism"* | Not found in David's words |

⚠⚠ **Three of the four come from one document by a different author, which does not mention David anywhere.** That is how the attribution happened, and it is correctable.

**And two things David says repeatedly appear on none of the three lists:** the Bank of England (measure 7) and publicly funded charities (measure 10).
