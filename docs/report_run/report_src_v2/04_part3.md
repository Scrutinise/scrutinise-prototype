# Part 3 · Do the twelve move together?


## 3 · Do the twelve measures have to move together?

**The programme's central structural claim is that they do.** Undo any one, and the others are still standing to reproduce the effect complained of — so a government proceeding one Bill at a time will be defeated by whatever it has not yet reached. **That is the argument for one omnibus Act, and everything about delivery follows from it.**

**It is tested here, three ways.**

The analysis was run twelve times, once per measure, **each run blind to the other eleven.** No run was told what any other run found. That matters for reading what follows: where twelve independent runs converge, the convergence means something, because it was not designed in.

⚠ **What follows is a test, not a verdict.** Its limits are at the end and they are real.

---

### Test one: do the measures touch the same law?

If the measures interlock as claimed, the twelve runs should keep landing on the same Acts.

**Across all twelve, 23 Acts of Parliament were cited. Nineteen were cited by exactly one measure.**

| Act | Measures touching it |
|---|---|
| **Equality Act 2010** | **Equality Act · DEI · gender self-ID** — three measures |
| Equality Act 2006 | Equality Act · DEI |
| Constitutional Reform Act 2005 | Supreme Court · Great Repeal |
| Human Rights Act 1998 | HRA · Great Repeal |

And at the level of individual sections, **seven shared provisions in the entire programme** — six of which are the same pair of measures.

**Strip out the two overlaps that are David's Programme restating itself** (the Great Repeal contains the other measures by definition) **and the entire cross-measure overlap in the statute book is three measures sharing the Equality Act.**

---

### ⚠⚠ Finding one: three of the twelve measures are one measure

**The Equality Act, DEI practice in the civil service, and gender self-identification all converge on the same statute.** The first two share **six sections and eight court judgments** between them.

**This was not designed in.** The three were built separately, from separate statements of David's, on separate days. **They converged because the law does.**

What that means practically:

- **DEI has no separate legislative existence.** Its evidence is the Equality Act measure's evidence. Drafted as a distinct Bill it either duplicates the Equality Act repeal or contradicts it.
- **Gender self-identification is resolved by litigation rather than legislation**, and what remains of it sits inside the same Act.
- **So this is one workstream with three faces, not three measures.**

⚠ **The programme is more accurately described as ten measures than twelve.** It removes two Bills from a programme whose main constraint is parliamentary time.

---

### ⚠ Finding two: outside that cluster, the measures are markedly separable

**Nineteen of twenty-three Acts are touched by one measure only.** The Bank of England Act appears in the Bank of England measure and nowhere else. The Sentencing Council's founding provisions appear in that measure and nowhere else. The charities framework appears in the charities measure and nowhere else.

**On this evidence, most of David's Programme could be legislated one measure at a time without the remainder defeating it** — which is the opposite of the claim the omnibus Act rests on.

⚠⚠ **This is the weaker of the two findings and must be read with its limit attached.** Absence of shared citations is not absence of dependency. Two measures can be deeply interdependent without citing the same Act — through convention, through institutional practice, or through something the search had no reason to look for. **Each run was asked about its own measure. Nothing was hunting for cross-links.**

**So this is evidence that the measures are separable in the statute book. It is not proof that they are separable in politics.** The first finding is strong because convergence was not designed in. The second is suggestive because nothing was looking for it. Both are stated at their true weight.

---

### Test two: where the courts join measures the statute book does not

Ninety judgments were cited across the twelve runs. Seventeen appear in more than one measure — **and the groupings do not follow the statutory ones.**

| Shared | Measures | What the shared cases are about |
|---|---|---|
| 3 judgments | **Quangos · civil service · DEI** | What public authorities may lawfully do, and who answers for it |
| 8 judgments | Equality Act · DEI | The Equality Act cluster again |
| 4 judgments | Quangos · civil service | Delegated decisions and lawful authority |
| *For Women Scotland* | Supreme Court · gender self-ID | The Court doing the thing David's Programme wants done |

⚠⚠ **The quangos / civil service / DEI cluster is a real interlock that the statute book does not show.** Those three share no Act at all, but they share judgments about what public bodies may lawfully do and who is accountable for it. **That is the accountability question underneath all three** — and it is the strongest evidence in this document *for* the Programme's structural claim. It was found by the courts, not by the drafters.

**So the honest answer to "do they interlock" is: not through the statutes, and partly through the case law.** Which is the same observation as Part 2.1: **what binds these measures together lives in the judgments, not in the Acts.**

---

### ⚠⚠ Test three: where the analysis disagreed with David's Programme

The twelve runs were not asked to agree. **On four of twelve measures the analysis proposed a different remedy from David's,** in its own drafted plan, having read the record.

**These split into two classes, and the difference matters.**

#### (a) Reached on material not supplied

| Measure | David's remedy | What was proposed instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank of England** | Reverse operational independence | It diagnosed the 1998 Act as having *"reclassified the inherently political and distributional choices of monetary policy as a technical exercise"*. Its alternative is to attack **the legitimacy of the consensus** rather than the Act: make ministers argue in public that interest rates are a political choice, so that the case for taking the decision back is won before the law is changed |
| **Charities** | Stop publicly funded charities campaigning | *"Fix accountability for the funding decision with the grantor, not by creating a new blanket ban"*. Its alternative reaches the same result without legislation: the accounting officer who signs off the grant is already personally answerable for the spending, so attach the campaigning condition to the grant and the existing accountability does the work |

⚠ **Neither alternative was seeded** — that is, neither was put into the analysis as an input. For the Bank, only the Act that conferred independence was supplied. For charities the available instruments were supplied, but not any preference between them, because David names none.

**Charities is the sharper of the two.** Given a measure with no stated instrument, the analysis did not invent one for David. It found where the accountability actually sits and proposed the narrowest thing that would reach it.

#### (b) Where the counter-material was supplied, and built on

| Measure | What the analysis did with the counter-evidence |
|---|---|
| **Civil service** | Opened on the target: *"The obstacle is not the Constitutional Reform and Governance Act 2010…"* — then proposed forcing a direct argument about the 1854 settlement, instead of a repeal it calls legally ineffective |
| **Gender self-ID** | Opened on *For Women Scotland* having settled the meaning of "sex" in the Equality Act, and proposed **using existing powers to enforce the law as it now stands — no new legislation at all** |
| **Judicial review** | Opened on the government's own review *"finding no systemic judicial overreach"* before setting out the case anyway. ⚠ Note that a process does not have to amount to systemic overreach to be abused, or to impose unnecessary cost and delay — and that is the case the measure actually rests on |
| **Supreme Court** | *"Re-establish parliamentary supremacy over constitutional interpretation not by abolishing the court"* — but by creating a standing parliamentary committee to scrutinise and correct judicial interpretation of constitutional statutes |

⚠⚠ **That material was in the inputs, so these four are not independent findings and are not presented as any.** What they show is narrower and still worth printing: **given evidence that cut against the measure, the analysis did not discard it, bury it, or argue round it. It reorganised the remedy around it and said so in the first line.**

**That is the behaviour this whole report is asking of a legislative process** — which is why the line between (a) and (b) is drawn here rather than quietly smudged.

---

### What this section hands back

1. **The interlock claim is not supported in the statute book.** Most of David's Programme is separable.
2. **It is partly supported in the case law**, in one specific place: quangos, the civil service and DEI share judgments about public authority accountability while sharing no statute. ⚠ **If the omnibus argument is right, this is where it is right** — and it is a narrower, more defensible version of the claim than the one now made.
3. **Three measures are one measure.** The Equality Act strand should be drafted once.
4. **On four measures the analysis reached a different remedy** — on two of them from material that was not supplied.

### ⚠ What this test cannot tell you

- **Whether measures depend on each other other than through shared law.** Shared citations are a proxy for dependency, not a measure of it.
- **Whether the nineteen single-measure Acts are genuinely isolated**, or merely searched in isolation. A cross-measure search has never been run and is the obvious next thing to do.
- ⚠ **Six of the eleven runs were cut short at six research questions by a spending limit.** Some of what those runs record as gaps are **questions never asked, not questions asked and unanswered** — and the output does not distinguish the two. **No count in this section is a limit on what can be found.** It is its reach on a capped run.
- **Whether there are strong strategic or tactical political reasons to legislate in one "big bang"** — to prevent the opposition organising, and to speed the process up — **or whether rolling it all together makes the whole programme vulnerable to a problem with any one part of it.** That is a political judgement, and nothing here tests it.
