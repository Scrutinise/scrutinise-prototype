# What is in this volume

**This volume carries the supporting material.** It is not argument. It is the evidence the argument in Volumes 1 and 2 rests on, set out at length so that any statement in those volumes can be checked back to its source.

**The numbering runs on from Volumes 1 and 2.** Volume 1 holds Parts 1 to 5, Volume 2 holds Parts 6 and 7, and this volume holds Parts 8 and 9.

**Part 8 · Who is for and against the proposed measures** — for each of the twelve measures, the organisations and named individuals who will argue about it, and what they have already said.

- **8.1 · The stakeholder map** — think tanks, campaign groups, professional and legal bodies, unions, regulators, peers and former judges, academics, bloggers, podcasters and committee witnesses, split between those with a published position and those who will certainly respond but have not yet said how.
- **8.2 · The parliamentary record** — the divisions and early day motions the corpus returned, with an account of why that record is thinner than it looks.

**Part 9 · The research** — every source retrieved and read, measure by measure, under the question it was retrieved to answer.

# What David said, and how to search it

**There is a fourth file, and it is not printed here because printing it would run to about fifteen hundred pages.**

**WHAT_DAVID_SAID.md** holds **2,764 passages from David's recorded words, across 254 of the 285 recordings held**, grouped by measure and in date order, each with a link that starts playing at the second it was said. It was built by searching every recording in the corpus against 102 terms drawn from the twelve measures.

**It is a plain text file, and that is deliberate.** It can be given to any AI assistant as a document to search, which is the fastest way to answer *"where did he say that?"* — the file carries the date, the recording title, the timestamp and the passage, so an answer can always be taken back to the recording.

⚠ **A term matching is not agreement, disagreement, or a proposal.** These are passages that mention the subject. Read the passage.

⚠⚠ **Almost none of it has been cross-checked.** The corpus was built to hold two independent transcripts of each recording so that quotations could be checked against each other. It holds two for three recordings out of 285. **2,728 of the 2,764 passages — 98.7% — have been read once, by one engine.** Each entry says which it is, in words, so that an absent mark is never read as agreement. Where two transcripts disagree the entry is marked and both are printed; in one recording one engine renders a name as "Israeli" where the other renders it "Disraeli".

**Any quotation taken from it should be checked against the recording before it is used in public.** The timestamp is given so that it can be.
