# Limitations of this analysis

*Prepared 9 September 2026. CCW-B18 §9. For CCW to place; nothing below is edited into the client
document by CC.*

---

The research behind this paper was assembled from a corpus of United Kingdom primary and secondary
legislation, court judgments, parliamentary debates, select committee material, government guidance
and departmental impact assessments. What that corpus can and cannot answer is set out below, in
each case with the figure that establishes it. Where a limit is a matter of degree rather than of
kind, the figure is given rather than a characterisation of it.

## What the search can reach, and what it cannot

The corpus is searched by two methods at once — matching on words, and matching on meaning — across
five bodies of material: legislation, case law, debates, committee papers and government guidance.
Both methods were confirmed working on the day the analysis in this paper was produced.

That confirmation matters, because it has not always been true. One chapter of an earlier draft was
produced while both search methods were misconfigured. Eighteen searches returned nothing; the run
nonetheless completed and reported success, and the resulting chapter cited two statutory
instruments and one judgment. The same analysis re-run with search working returned half as much
again — 101 sources against 73, and 241 documents examined at the opening stage against 48. The
affected chapter has been re-run and the figures in this paper come from the re-run.

A check has since been written that stops a run from starting at all when either search method is
misconfigured, and a second that records whether the search services were actually reached during a
run, so that this failure announces itself rather than being discovered in the finished text. **At
the date of this paper the first of those is in the code and not yet in service.** Until it is,
confirmation remains a manual step taken before each run.

The corpus does not contain everything, and three absences bear directly on this paper:

- **BAILII, the principal free repository of British case law, is not used at all.** No judgment
  text has been taken from it and no request has been made to it. Case law here comes from the
  National Archives' own published judgments.
- **Early day motion signatures are not held.** The record contains 60,995 early day motions and,
  for each, the single member who tabled it. Parliament publishes 2,125,547 signatories across those
  motions — an average of thirty-five per motion. None of them is held. Where this paper reports who
  has supported a proposition in Parliament, it is drawing on divisions and debates, never on the
  signature record, which is the largest single body of evidence about backbench opinion and is
  absent.
- **Government publications outside legislation.gov.uk are not ingested.** Post-implementation
  reviews in particular are frequently published on gov.uk alone, and that route has never been
  taken in.

## Where the record begins

Several parts of the record start later than a reader would assume, and a count that appears to show
absence may only show that the record has not begun.

| Record | Begins |
|---|---|
| Recorded divisions of the House of Commons | 9 March 2016 |
| Recorded divisions of the House of Lords | 24 November 1999 |
| Judgments of the courts of England and Wales | 2003 |
| Judgments of the Scottish courts | 1999 |

A count of how often a member has voted on a subject is therefore a count since 2016 and not a
count over a career.

## Completeness of the corpus, stated as counts

No part of this corpus is claimed to be complete, and two figures are worth stating precisely
because larger versions of them have circulated.

**Impact assessments.** The corpus holds **1,169 impact assessments** across 869 distinct
documents. A figure of 18,700 has been used elsewhere; that is the count of *sections* within those
assessments, not of assessments. Of the 1,169, **71 are genuine post-implementation reviews** — that
is, an assessment of what a measure actually did after it was in force. Around a thousand sections
carry the heading "Post-implementation review", but almost all of them are the standard front-sheet
question *"Will the policy be reviewed?"* asked of a measure before it commences. A promise to
review is not a review.

Two gaps in the feed, covering 2008–2016 and 2024–2025, were re-checked against the publisher in
September 2026 and are still real.

**Reviews that were promised and not delivered.** Of 231 assessments that promised a review, gave a
due year, and named an instrument, **196 are past their due date with no review deposited**. This is
a *floor* on the number of unreviewed measures and not the number itself: a review published on
gov.uk alone would not appear in this corpus, and that route has never been ingested. It should be
read as "at least 196", never as "196".

## The citator

The paper checks the authorities it cites against a citation index built from the courts' own
published markup. Its two halves are of very different strength and should be relied on differently.

**References from judgments to legislation are strong.** 1,288,630 such references across 52,861
judgments, of which 99.0% resolve to an identified provision. These come from tagging applied by the
publisher rather than inferred from the text.

**Judicial treatment — whether a case has been followed, distinguished, doubted or overruled — is
weak, and has no measured error rate at all.** There are 1,749 treatment records. **None has been
validated.** A sample has been prepared for scoring and, until it is scored, every statement in this
paper about how a case has been treated should be read as unverified. Three classes of known error
have already been identified by inspection: a hypothetical clause in a judgment read as a holding;
a word used in a different sense (a person being "distinguishable" from another is not a case being
"distinguished"); and, in one instance, a quotation from a Victorian statute recorded as overruling
a section of the Human Rights Act 1998.

Four further limits apply to the citation index:

- **It sees citations inward only.** It can say which later judgments cite a given case; it cannot
  say what that case itself cited.
- **Most citations are not treatments, and are correctly left unclassified.** 16,455 of 18,211
  candidate passages produce no treatment record. A refusal is the right answer where a case is
  merely mentioned.
- **Whether a cited authority is itself held in the corpus has three states, not two.** 24.1% are
  held, 54.9% are not, and for **21.0% it cannot be determined**, because the same case is commonly
  cited under two different references. Treating the undetermined fifth as "not held" would understate
  what is available.
- **Nothing in this system records that a provision or a case is no longer good law, and no such
  statement is made anywhere in this paper.** There is no field capable of holding that conclusion,
  and it is not one this analysis is in a position to reach.

## The record of who has taken which position

16,196 recorded positions of members and organisations have been extracted from the corpus. **They
are not searchable, and they are not published in this paper as findings.** Read by hand against
their sources, 44% were found to be wrong. That is not a rate at which a register of who supports
what can be put in front of a reader.

⚠ **That 44% is a rate of misinterpretation, not of invention, and the distinction determines how a
reader should check it.** Where a quotation is recorded, it can be found in the source document it is
attributed to **98.4% of the time**. The passage quoted is almost always genuinely there and
genuinely said by the person named. What is unreliable is the further claim that the passage shows
that person holding the position ascribed to them. The question to put to any such entry is therefore
not *is this quotation real* but *does this quotation show what it is said to show* — which is why
every entry in the supporting material carries the quotation itself rather than a summary of it.

Where this paper names a person as having taken a position, the position is accompanied by the words
they used and the place they used them, and it should be treated as a candidate for confirmation
rather than as an established fact. Anything that could not be confirmed against the quotation has
been removed rather than qualified.

**A second limit is more serious for a paper organised by measure.** Connecting a broad proposal to
the specific parliamentary business bearing on it is done by matching phrases, and on the twelve
measures examined here the result does not support a per-measure table of supporters and opponents:

- **Two of the twelve** connect to a recorded division, where the full voting record is held; these
  return 254 and 189 members respectively.
- **Eight connect only to an early day motion**, and for those the corpus returns exactly one name —
  the member who tabled it — for the reason given above.
- **Two connect to nothing at all.**
- **Ten of the twelve connections rest on a two- or three-word phrase**, and several are simply the
  wrong subject: the proposal on arm's-length bodies matched a motion on advertising standards; the
  proposal on the civil service and the proposal on diversity practice both matched the same motion,
  which is about civil service pensions.

Accordingly, no per-measure register of supporters and opponents is offered in this paper.

## Cost and benefit

**No costing has been produced for any proposal in this paper, and the reason is a gap in the
system rather than a judgement about the proposals.** Across the whole database, none of the 119
drafted actions carries a cost range, and there is one cost line in total. The machinery to produce
costings exists and has been tested; nothing connects it to a proposal. On each of the twelve
measures examined, the heading "Cost and duration" is empty.

Where this paper asks what an existing measure has cost, the answer is frequently that no figure
exists. For the public sector equality duty, three separate absences were established: **no impact
assessment of it is held, no post-implementation review of it was carried out, and no statistical
series measures it.** That is a finding about the state of the public record and not a failure of
the search.

## What this paper's own analysis was checked against

Each proposal was tested against a fixed set of questions about whether it amounts to a strategy —
whether the problem is stated as a problem, whether the diagnosis identifies what must be defeated,
whether the guiding policy rules anything out, and whether the actions bear on the obstacle named.

**Those tests were, until 9 September 2026, being applied to an incomplete statement of each
proposal.** The text placed before them omitted the diagnosis, the identified obstacle, the guiding
policy and the plan, and consisted of a title and two lists. The failures the tests recorded were
accurate readings of what they were shown and are not reliable assessments of the proposals
themselves. This has been corrected, and the assessments in this paper's appendices are being
reproduced against the complete text. **No verdict from a test run before that date should be
quoted.**

## Two further gaps found while preparing this paper

- On each of the twelve measures, between six and eleven retrieved sources are held against the
  proposal but appear under no heading in the research record, and so are not visible to a reader
  working through it. Approximately ninety-five sources across the twelve are affected. They are
  present in the data and are listed in the appendix.
- The question "what is devolved about this?" returned nothing on any of the twelve measures,
  including those where the devolution settlements are directly engaged and where the relevant
  provisions of the Scotland Act 1998, the Northern Ireland Act 1998 and the Government of Wales Act
  2006 have been verified by hand for this paper. The devolution material in this paper was assembled
  by hand and not by search.

## The standing rule applied to citations

Five fabricated citations were produced during the research for the first draft — references with
plausible numbers, plausible authors and plausible dates, to documents that do not exist. Each had a
real near-neighbour: a genuine paper with a different number, a genuine lecture by a different
person. **No citation appears in this paper unless it has been confirmed against a second source or
found in the corpus.**
