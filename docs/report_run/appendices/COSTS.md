## How to read it

- **Every figure is a range, and the width is information.** A narrow range means the basis
  is firm; a wide one means it is not. A single number would be false precision.
- **Every line states its basis.** A line whose basis could not be stated was dropped, not
  softened — and the drops are listed, because a silently shorter table reads as a cheaper
  policy.
- ⚠ **A cited benchmark and a reasoned estimate are marked differently and must not be**
  **conflated.** A Green Book unit cost and a plausible guess look identical once both are
  numbers in a table. Lines marked *reasoned* rest on the model's own assumptions.
- ⚠ **Nothing here is totalled.** Adding implementation, enforcement and friction across
  measures would produce a number with no owner and no basis.
