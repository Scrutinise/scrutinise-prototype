# Part 2 §4.8 — Who else has said what: the external viewpoint register

**Draft 1, 2 Sep 2026, CCW.**

⚠ **A note on method, stated because it is a finding about our own process.** This section did not
exist in the first plan. It was added after the proposer's own reading surfaced two authorities —
Lord Sumption and Dominic Grieve KC — that our research had missed entirely, because we had
researched *the law* and never asked *who else has published on this*. Every item below was found
in a handful of ordinary searches. **The lesson is recorded here rather than hidden: the commentary
sweep is cheap, high-yield, and was nearly omitted.**

**This is not a literature review and does not claim completeness.** It is what a competent
searcher finds quickly, set out so the proposers can say what we have missed.

## WS-01 · Human Rights Act 1998 and the ECHR

The best-covered measure by a wide margin. Four substantial positions, set out in §4.6.

| Who | Position | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Lord Wolfson KC**, Shadow Attorney General | Withdrawal justified; **warns against a Bill of Rights modelled on the Convention**; leaving would not be a "factory reset" | Advice to the Leader of the Opposition |
| **Lord Sumption**, former Justice of the Supreme Court | Objects to Strasbourg's "living instrument" doctrine, not the text; **replace with a domestic instrument that looks like the Convention** | Former member of the court the programme would abolish |
| **Prosperity Institute** (Braverman & Dampier) | Withdrawal plus a Case Law Review Commission under a sunset clause | The proposer's own collaborators |
| **Dominic Grieve KC**, former Attorney General | Against withdrawal, but **concedes domestic protection would survive it** | The strongest available opposition |

**Also relevant and not yet worked:** Sumption's 2019 Reith Lectures and *Trials of the State*
argue at length that law has expanded into territory belonging to politics. ⚠ **That is the thesis's
own diagnosis, made from the bench, six years before the thesis.** It is the single most useful
piece of corroboration still unmined, and it belongs in Part 1 rather than here.

## WS-04 · Equality Act 2010

⚠⚠ **Two findings here materially change what the register rows mean.**

### 1. The repeal of s.149 is already announced Conservative policy

Kemi Badenoch, in a speech at the **Institute for Government on 9 June 2026**, announced that a
Conservative government would **repeal the public sector equality duty in its entirety while
retaining the Act's substantive anti-discrimination protections.** ✔ Verified; she draws the
distinction herself, in terms, and names Reform UK's whole-Act repeal as the position she is arguing
against. Her words, and the three-position map they produce, are in §4.9 Part E.

⚠⚠ **And a hard correction that changes what these rows mean.** The Equality Act 2010 does not
extend to Northern Ireland save for three provisions named in s.217(3), and **s.149 is not one of
them. The public sector equality duty has never applied in Northern Ireland.** A Westminster Bill
repealing it would change nothing there. The nearest analogue is s.75 of the Northern Ireland Act
1998 — differently framed, differently enforced, and derived from the Belfast/Good Friday Agreement.
Set out precisely in §4.9 Part D.

**Why this matters more than it first appears.** It is the *same* split we found between Wolfson and
Sumption on the ECHR, appearing again on a different measure: **abolish the machinery, keep the
substance** versus **repeal the Act.** The thesis is on the second side of that line and the
official Opposition is on the first.

**So the register's Equality Act rows need a column they do not currently have:** is the target the
whole Act, or s.149 and the duties it creates? Those are different Bills with different consequential
tails, and the difference is now a live political question rather than a drafting nicety.

There is published pushback from the same side of the argument — the "amend, don't abolish" case —
which should be set out beside it rather than against it.

### 2. ⚠⚠ Part of what the thesis objects to has already been decided by the courts

**For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers [2025] UKSC 16.** The Supreme Court held
unanimously that "sex" in the Equality Act means biological sex. The Equality and Human Rights
Commission has since been working on consequential guidance; the House of Commons Library has
published a briefing on the judgment.

**This bears directly on register row SP-10**, where the objection is to gender self-identification.
That row already carried a warning that self-identification was never enacted in Great Britain. It
now carries a second and larger one: **the position the thesis objects to has been substantially
resolved by litigation, in the direction the thesis wants, without any legislation at all.**

⚠ **That is a finding the proposers should welcome and should also find uncomfortable**, and the
report must give both halves. It supports the diagnosis — the courts did move. It complicates the
remedy — because if the objectionable reading has already gone, what remains to be repealed, and is
repeal still the instrument for it?

**It is also the clearest available demonstration of why this report exists.** A proposal drafted
against a legal position that has since changed will fail, and only checking the current record
catches that.

### Opposition and evidence base, not yet worked

- The **Equality and Human Rights Commission** — the statutory body, with a stated position
- The **House of Lords Select Committee** work on the public sector equality duty — a ready-made
  evidence base on whether the duty achieves what it was designed to achieve
- ⚠ Neither has been read. Both should be before this chapter is written.

## WS-05 · CRAG 2010 Part 1 · the civil service

The register's weakest measure for external commentary — **until you look, and then it is the
best-served of the three.**

### The Maude Review is directly on point and nobody has cited it

**The Independent Review of Governance and Accountability in the Civil Service, by Lord Maude of
Horsham, published 13 November 2023** — a former Cabinet Office minister — examines precisely the
question the thesis raises: who is accountable for what in the civil service, and whether the present
structure works.

⚠⚠ **But it does not reach the thesis's conclusion, and an earlier version of this draft implied it
did.** The review expressly proceeds "on the basis that the UK's current system of a permanent and
politically impartial Civil Service will be maintained." It proposes major governance and
appointments changes and **stops short of repeal.** ⚠ *An earlier draft also dated it 2024. Both
errors are corrected here and recorded in §4.9 Part C rather than quietly fixed.*

**That makes it harder for the programme than opposition would be**, and more useful: Maude is not a
defender of the machine. The question it puts is not "are you right?" but **what does Maude know that
makes him stop where he stops.**

**Responses exist from across the spectrum and are all findable:**

| Source | Framing |
|---|---|
| **Institute for Government** | Argues the review should lead to cross-party agreement on reform |
| **UCL Constitution Unit** | "Institutional fixes for political problems" — sceptical that structural change addresses the underlying issue |
| **Re:State (formerly Reform)** | Its own analysis of the review |
| **House of Commons committee**, Jan 2024 | Oral evidence on Civil Service Leadership and Reform |

⚠ **The UCL framing is the one the report most needs**, because it is the strongest available
objection to the thesis's whole method: that changing institutions does not fix problems that are
political in origin. That argument deserves to be stated at its strongest, not summarised away.

### And a discrepancy in the proposer's own words, flagged rather than corrected

In Part 2 of the thesis series the transcript has him referring to *"the secretary of the First
Division Civil Servants Association, Mark Sirota"*. **Mark Serwotka led the PCS union, not the FDA**,
whose general secretary is Dave Penman. Two different unions.

⚠ **This may be the transcript mishearing rather than the speaker mis-speaking**, and we cannot tell
from a machine transcript which. It is in the quote-check sheet for the ear pass. **It is flagged
because a report that quoted it uncorrected would put an error into his mouth in print** — and
because if it is his error rather than the machine's, he would rather know.

## What this section establishes

**On every one of the three measures, substantial published analysis exists that the programme has
not engaged with** — and on two of them, the most senior contributions cut in directions the thesis
has not anticipated.

That is not a criticism of the thesis. It is what scrutiny is for, and it is the clearest possible
demonstration of what this instrument does: **the proposal did not change, but what a reader now
knows about it has.**
