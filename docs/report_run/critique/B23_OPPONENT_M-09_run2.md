### 1. The statutory guidance is read down to be consistent with the Gender Recognition Act 2004, preserving the rights of GRC holders.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** The Gender Recognition Act 2004, s.9(1), and the principle that statutory guidance cannot override primary legislation.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The Minister for Women and Equalities to use the power under the Equality Act 2010 to issue statutory guidance clarifying that 'sex' means biological sex for the purposes of the Act...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan explicitly rejects amending primary legislation in favour of statutory guidance. Guidance is legally subordinate to the GRA's clear statement in s.9(1) that a GRC changes a person's sex 'for all purposes'. A court will therefore read the guidance as not applying to GRC holders to resolve the conflict between the two instruments.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation amending either the Equality Act 2010 to state it overrides s.9(1) GRA 2004 for its purposes, or amending the GRA 2004 itself. The proposal's 'ACTIONS' list this, but the 'PLAN' abandons it.

### 2. The guidance is disapplied in individual cases where its application would be a disproportionate breach of a person's ECHR rights.

**Mechanism:** `DISAPPLICATION`

**Rests on:** The Human Rights Act 1998, incorporating Articles 8 (right to private life) and 14 (prohibition of discrimination) of the ECHR.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The Cabinet Office to direct all government departments and public bodies to audit and update their policies and guidance in line with the new statutory guidance...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan directs public bodies to apply the guidance. As public authorities, their actions must be compatible with the ECHR. A court will find that rigidly applying the guidance to exclude, for example, a post-operative trans woman with a GRC from a service, is a disproportionate interference with her Article 8 rights. The guidance will be disapplied on a case-by-case basis, defeating the goal of legal certainty.

*What would close it:* The plan cannot close this route without repealing the Human Rights Act 1998. Any action by a public authority remains subject to ECHR proportionality review.

### 3. The guidance is challenged by judicial review as being ultra vires because it purports to resolve a conflict between two Acts of Parliament, a function reserved for the courts.

**Mechanism:** `OTHER` — Judicial review on grounds of illegality (ultra vires).

**Rests on:** The common law doctrine of ultra vires and the constitutional separation of powers.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> ...issue statutory guidance clarifying that 'sex' means biological sex for the purposes of the Act, including for single-sex services, and direct the EHRC to update its Code of Practice accordingly.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan uses guidance to resolve the tension between the Equality Act 2010 (as interpreted by the fictional FWS case) and the Gender Recognition Act 2004. This is a constitutional overreach. A Minister cannot use guidance to settle what is, in effect, a doctrine of implied repeal between two statutes. A court is likely to find the guidance unlawful for attempting to exercise a judicial function.

*What would close it:* Using primary legislation to amend the statutes directly. The plan's choice of guidance as the tool is the source of the vulnerability.

### 4. The protected characteristic of 'gender reassignment' is used to secure the same access rights that the guidance on 'sex' seeks to remove.

**Mechanism:** `PROCEDURAL_SURVIVAL`

**Rests on:** The Equality Act 2010, s.7 (Protected Characteristic of Gender Reassignment).

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The Minister for Women and Equalities to use the power under the Equality Act 2010 to issue statutory guidance clarifying that 'sex' means biological sex for the purposes of the Act...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan focuses exclusively on the definition of 'sex' and ignores the separate protected characteristic of 'gender reassignment'. A service provider who implements the guidance by excluding a trans person will still face a discrimination claim under s.7. They would then have to prove the exclusion was a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim, which is the exact same complex, case-by-case legal test the proposal sought to avoid.

*What would close it:* The plan would need to amend the Equality Act 2010 to remove or substantially limit the protections for gender reassignment, which it does not propose to do.
