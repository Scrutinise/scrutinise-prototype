**The opponent's line:** The proposal fundamentally misunderstands the legal landscape. ### 1. The underlying common law duty of rational decision-making will be used to challenge decisions that have a disproportionate impact on protected groups, replacing a predictable process duty with a less constrained, more substantive ground of review.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** The common law duty to take into account all relevant considerations and the principles of Wednesbury unreasonableness.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan's actions focus exclusively on repealing the statutory duty and preserving other parts of 'the Act', leaving the common law untouched. The proposal to issue new guidance on 'remaining equality obligations under other parts of the Equality Act 2010' completely overlooks this more fundamental source of obligation.

**⚠⚠ **the plan makes it worse**** — The PSED provides a 'safe harbour' for public bodies: if the process is followed correctly, the substantive decision is hard to challenge on these grounds. By repealing it, the proposal removes this procedural shield and exposes the substantive merits of decisions to direct challenge as irrational or for failure to consider their manifest equality impacts. This invites a more intrusive, unpredictable, and costly form of litigation.

*What would close it:* The Bill would need to include a broad ouster clause attempting to prevent judicial review of public authority decisions on grounds of their equality-related impacts, which would be legally and constitutionally fraught.

### 2. Decisions made without regard to their equality impact will be challenged as breaches of the Human Rights Act 1998, specifically the prohibition on discrimination under Article 14 of the ECHR.

**Mechanism:** `ENTRENCHED_ELSEWHERE`

**Rests on:** Section 6 of the Human Rights Act 1998, which makes it unlawful for a public authority to act in a way which is incompatible with a Convention right, including Article 14 (prohibition of discrimination) read with other articles.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan is narrowly focused on the Equality Act 2010. The action to 'explicitly state that other substantive anti-discrimination provisions of the Act remain in force' is silent on parallel obligations under other primary legislation, such as the Human Rights Act.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan takes no steps to amend or repeal the Human Rights Act. Therefore, a powerful, entirely separate statutory route for challenging discriminatory actions by public bodies remains fully intact. The legal analysis required for an HRA claim (e.g., proportionality) is at least as rigorous as that required by the PSED.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation to amend or repeal the relevant sections of the Human Rights Act 1998, a constitutionally significant act far beyond the scope of this proposal.

### 3. The repeal will be rendered ineffective in Scotland and Wales, where the devolved governments will refuse legislative consent and can use their own powers to maintain equivalent duties on devolved public bodies.

**Mechanism:** `DISAPPLICATION`

**Rests on:** The Sewel Convention and the devolution settlements under the Scotland Act 1998 and Government of Wales Act 2006. The specific equality duties for Scotland and Wales are already distinct and set out in devolved secondary legislation.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The proposal is framed as a single UK-wide policy, with actions led by UK government departments like the Cabinet Office and DLUHC. The plan contains no reference to the devolved administrations or the need for Legislative Consent Motions.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan has no strategy for the devolved nations. The Scottish and Welsh Governments will almost certainly refuse consent for the repeal, and while Westminster can technically proceed, it would be a major constitutional breach. More practically, they can use their extensive powers over devolved public services to re-impose similar or identical duties, effectively disapplying the repeal in their territories and creating a fractured, inconsistent legal framework across Great Britain.

*What would close it:* Securing Legislative Consent Motions from the Scottish Parliament and the Senedd, which is politically inconceivable, or redrafting the Bill to apply only to England, which would defeat the stated aim of a comprehensive reform.
