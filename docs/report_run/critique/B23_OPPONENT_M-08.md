### 1. The 'legally authoritative' guidance will be declared unlawful by the courts.

**Mechanism:** `DISAPPLICATION`

**Rests on:** The constitutional principle that executive guidance cannot alter the meaning of a statute as interpreted by the courts (the doctrine of ultra vires). Case law, such as R (Baker) v Secretary of State for Communities and Local Government [2008] EWCA Civ 141, establishes the substantive, rigorous nature of the 'due regard' duty in s.149 of the Equality Act 2010.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The Cabinet Office... should issue revised, legally authoritative guidance on the interpretation and application of Section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 for the Civil Service.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan has no mechanism to make executive guidance 'authoritative' over judicial precedent. The guidance will be challenged at the first opportunity via judicial review of a decision made under it, and the courts will likely find it misstates the law. The plan does not amend the primary legislation, which is the only way to close this route.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation amending Section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 to redefine the scope of the duty.

### 2. The statutory duty to 'advance equality of opportunity' will be used to justify proactive DEI measures, irrespective of the new guidance.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** The plain text of the Equality Act 2010, specifically Section 149(1)(b) which requires due regard to 'advance equality of opportunity', and Section 149(3) which defines this as including removing disadvantages and taking steps to meet needs.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> This guidance must explicitly state that 'due regard' primarily means avoiding unlawful discrimination and promoting genuine equality of opportunity, rather than mandating... extensive proactive DEI initiatives.

**◐ partly closed** — The guidance attempts to create a distinction between 'promoting genuine equality of opportunity' (which it permits) and 'extensive proactive DEI initiatives' (which it discourages). This is a semantic, not a legal, distinction. A department can argue that its DEI initiatives are necessary to fulfil the statutory requirement to 'advance equality of opportunity' as defined in s.149(3), thereby reading down the guidance's prohibition. The plan makes such arguments harder but does not eliminate them.

*What would close it:* Amending or repealing Sections 149(1)(b) and 149(3) of the Equality Act 2010.

### 3. The Civil Service Commission will assert its statutory independence to maintain its existing recruitment principles, which already balance merit with diversity.

**Mechanism:** `ENTRENCHED_ELSEWHERE`

**Rests on:** The Constitutional Reform and Governance Act 2010 (CRAG), which establishes the Civil Service Commission as an independent body with a primary duty to uphold selection on merit through fair and open competition. The Commission's own published Recruitment Principles already interpret this as requiring outreach to a diverse field.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The Civil Service Commission should update its recruitment principles and guidance to align with the narrowed interpretation of the PSED...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan 'asks' the Commission to update its principles. As an independent statutory body, the Commission is not required to follow Cabinet Office guidance on how to interpret its own duties under CRAG. It can, and likely will, argue that its existing principles already correctly balance merit and diversity in a way that is consistent with its statutory mandate, and that the new, narrower guidance is not binding upon it.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation amending the Constitutional Reform and Governance Act 2010 to make the Commission subject to ministerial direction on the interpretation of its recruitment principles.

### 4. Repealing internal DEI policies and guidance creates a legal vacuum, forcing decision-makers to fall back on the unconstrained, judicially-interpreted PSED, potentially leading to more cautious and expansive applications than before.

**Mechanism:** `OTHER` — Unintended consequence of removing a compliance framework while leaving the underlying duty intact.

**Rests on:** The continuing, non-delegable nature of the statutory duty in s.149 of the Equality Act 2010. Without specific internal guidance, individual managers and departments must make their own assessment of how to show 'due regard', likely leading them to adopt a risk-averse approach based on the broad requirements set out in case law.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The Civil Service Commission should update its recruitment principles and guidance... The Treasury should review and revise funding allocations and performance metrics... to ensure they do not incentivise or reward expansive DEI programs...

**⚠⚠ **the plan makes it worse**** — The plan aims to remove the framework of 'expansive DEI programs'. In doing so, it removes the very mechanisms that channel and manage compliance with the PSED. This does not remove the underlying statutory duty. It leaves thousands of individual civil service managers personally responsible for demonstrating they have had 'due regard' to the broad, court-defined equality aims. To avoid legal risk, they are likely to revert to box-ticking, commissioning external advice, and documenting every possible equality consideration, leading to more bureaucracy and cost, not less.
