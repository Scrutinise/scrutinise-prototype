### 1. A charity will argue that the definitions of 'campaigning activities' and 'directly oppose' are uncertain and must be read narrowly to avoid infringing on its legitimate work and freedom of expression. For example, providing critical evidence to a select committee or publishing research that reveals the negative impacts of a policy is not 'campaigning' that 'directly opposes' policy, but rather fulfilling its charitable purpose.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** Public law principles requiring legal certainty for restrictions, principles of contractual interpretation, and the interpretive pressure of Article 10 of the European Convention on Human Rights.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The action to 'develop a template for explicit grant conditions' with 'clear definitions of “campaigning activities” and “directly oppose”' concedes that these terms are ambiguous and require definition, opening the door to legal challenges over the scope and application of whatever definition is chosen.

**◐ partly closed** — The plan attempts to close this by creating 'clear definitions'. However, drafting a definition that is both comprehensive enough to be effective and precise enough to be legally certain is exceptionally difficult. Any definition will be subject to interpretation by the courts, who will likely favour a narrow construction that protects the charity's ability to speak.

*What would close it:* A statutory definition of 'prohibited, publicly-funded campaigning' would be harder to challenge, though still subject to interpretation and human rights scrutiny.

### 2. A charity will argue that its primary legal duty is to its charitable objects, as defined in its governing instrument and the Charities Act. If a government policy harms its beneficiaries, its trustees are bound by charity law to act in their best interests, which can include campaigning against that policy. They will argue this primary duty overrides a conflicting contractual grant condition, making the condition unenforceable.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** The Charities Act 2011 and the common law duties of charity trustees to act in the best interests of their charity's purposes.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The entire proposal rests on the enforceability of a grant condition. It does not account for the possibility that this condition could directly conflict with the pre-existing, and arguably higher, legal duties of the charity's trustees under charity law.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan makes no provision for this conflict of duties. It assumes the grant condition will be paramount. A court may well decide that trustees cannot lawfully bind their charity to a contract term that prevents them from fulfilling their core legal purpose.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation explicitly stating that acceptance of a grant legally subordinates a charity's general duties to the specific contractual conditions for the duration of the grant.

### 3. A charity will structure its finances to isolate the government grant for service delivery, while using its privately-raised, unrestricted funds to finance all campaigning and advocacy staff and activities. It will then argue that it is in full compliance with the condition, as no public money was spent on campaigning. Proving otherwise would require a forensic level of audit that grant-makers are unlikely to be able to conduct.

**Mechanism:** `PROCEDURAL_SURVIVAL`

**Rests on:** The legal principle of the fungibility of money and standard accounting practices for restricted and unrestricted funds.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The guiding policy explicitly allows 'charities to campaign using other funds'. This is a deliberate policy choice that creates a straightforward and lawful route for charities to continue campaigning simply by allocating funds appropriately.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan is designed to permit this. It targets the use of specific public funds, not the charity's overall activity. This makes the policy largely symbolic and easy to circumvent through routine financial management.

*What would close it:* A condition that any recipient of public funds is barred from campaigning against the grantor, regardless of which funds are used. This would be a far more draconian and legally challengeable policy.

### 4. A charity will argue that the imposition of a new, restrictive 'anti-lobbying' clause is a breach of the legitimate expectation created by the long-standing Government Compact, which commits the government to 'recognise and support the independence of the sector, including its right within the law to campaign... irrespective of any funding relationship'.

**Mechanism:** `ENTRENCHED_ELSEWHERE`

**Rests on:** The Government Compact and the public law doctrine of legitimate expectation.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The proposal's own research cites The Compact and its explicit protection of the right to campaign 'irrespective of any funding relationship'. The plan to impose a contrary standard clause across government provides the basis for a legal challenge.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan does not mention The Compact or provide any justification for departing from its principles. It proceeds as if this long-standing political settlement does not exist, leaving the government's action vulnerable to a judicial review claim that it has frustrated a legitimate expectation without lawful reason.

*What would close it:* The government would need to formally and publicly resile from The Compact, explaining its reasons, before implementing this policy. This would be a politically significant act.
