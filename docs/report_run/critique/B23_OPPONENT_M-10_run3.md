### 1. The core terms of the proposed grant condition, 'campaigning activities' and 'directly oppose', will be interpreted narrowly by the courts, allowing charities to continue most forms of policy engagement by classifying them as 'evidence-based advocacy' or 'suggesting improvements' rather than direct opposition.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** General principles of contractual interpretation, including the contra proferentem rule, and the public law principle of legality requiring clear statutory language to curtail fundamental freedoms.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> This template should include clear definitions of 'campaigning activities' and 'directly oppose'.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan assumes that these terms can be defined with sufficient legal precision to be unchallengeable. In practice, any definition will create grey areas that a charity's lawyers will exploit. A court is likely to favour an interpretation that permits activity close to the line, especially where it relates to a charity's core purpose.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation that defines the prohibited activities with extreme specificity, although even this would be subject to judicial interpretation and human rights challenges.

### 2. A charity will argue that accepting the grant condition is incompatible with the trustees' overriding legal duty to act in the best interests of their beneficiaries, which may require campaigning against a harmful government policy. They will contend the clause is therefore void on public policy grounds.

**Mechanism:** `ENTRENCHED_ELSEWHERE`

**Rests on:** The common law and statutory duties of charity trustees under the Charities Act 2011, and the principles outlined in the Government's own 'Compact' with the third sector.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The approach is to make the departmental Accounting Officer—who is already personally responsible for the proper use of public money—explicitly answerable for ensuring grant conditions prevent public funds being used to lobby the grantor.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan operates at the level of contract and administrative guidance, which is subordinate to the fundamental duties of trustees established in statute and common law. It creates a conflict of duties for trustees, and a court or the Charity Tribunal could rule that their primary duty to their charitable objects prevails over a contractual term with a funder.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation amending the Charities Act 2011 to subordinate trustees' duties to the terms of public grant agreements, which would be a radical and highly controversial change to the basis of charity law.

### 3. The grant condition will be challenged as a disproportionate interference with the charity's right to freedom of expression under Article 10 of the ECHR, as enacted by the Human Rights Act 1998. A court may find the government's aim of controlling the use of its funds does not justify a broad restriction on political speech by a key part of civil society.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** Article 10 of the European Convention on Human Rights and the Human Rights Act 1998.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> Develop a template for explicit grant conditions, stipulating that public funds... cannot be used for campaigning activities that directly oppose the stated policies of the funding body.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan contains no assessment of its compatibility with the Human Rights Act. As the mandatory clause would be imposed by a public authority, it must be compliant with the ECHR. A legal challenge would force the government to justify the restriction as 'necessary in a democratic society', a high bar that a blanket contractual clause may fail to meet.

*What would close it:* The plan would need to include a detailed justification for why the restriction is proportionate, necessary to meet a pressing social need, and the least intrusive means of achieving its objective. Even then, it would remain vulnerable to challenge.

### 4. The proposal's focus on the 'funding body' creates a simple loophole: a charity funded by one department (e.g., DHSC) can use those funds to campaign against the policies of another department (e.g., Home Office), as the grant condition would not apply. This allows the activity to continue, merely displacing its target.

**Mechanism:** `DISAPPLICATION`

**Rests on:** A literal reading of the proposed grant condition's text.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> ...campaigning activities that directly oppose the stated policies of the funding body.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan's own wording limits the clause's application to the specific grantor-grantee relationship. This precision is a fatal flaw, as it fails to address campaigning against government policy more broadly, which is part of the problem statement. Charities can and will continue to campaign against policies of departments that do not fund them.

*What would close it:* The clause would need to be redrafted to prohibit campaigning against the policy of 'His Majesty's Government' as a whole, but this would make it far more vulnerable to the other challenges identified (breach of trustee duty, human rights) by dramatically broadening its scope.
