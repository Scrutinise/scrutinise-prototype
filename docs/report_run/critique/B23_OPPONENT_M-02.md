### 1. The common law duty of procedural fairness will be interpreted to require consideration of equality impacts, effectively recreating a PSED-like duty.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** The common law duty of public authorities to act fairly, which includes taking into account all relevant considerations (Wednesbury unreasonableness) and a duty to make inquiries where necessary.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan's actions focus exclusively on repealing the statutory duty and updating guidance on 'remaining equality obligations under other parts of the Equality Act 2010 and other relevant legislation'.

**⚠⚠ **the plan makes it worse**** — The plan does not address the common law at all. By repealing the specific, codified duty in s149, it removes a clear statutory framework and invites litigation to define the scope of the common law duty of fairness in its place. Courts, accustomed to the logic of the PSED for over a decade, are likely to import its principles into the common law, creating a new, less predictable, and judicially-led duty of consideration.

*What would close it:* The Bill would need to include a provision explicitly ousting or limiting the common law duty of fairness as it applies to equality considerations. This would be highly controversial and subject to restrictive interpretation by the courts.

### 2. The Human Rights Act 1998 will be used to compel public bodies to conduct pre-emptive analysis of policies to avoid discriminatory outcomes under ECHR Article 14.

**Mechanism:** `ENTRENCHED_ELSEWHERE`

**Rests on:** Section 6 of the Human Rights Act 1998, which makes it unlawful for a public authority to act incompatibly with a Convention right, particularly Article 14 (prohibition of discrimination) read with other rights (e.g., Article 8).

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan's entire focus is on the Equality Act 2010. The action to 'repeal Section 149 and Schedule 18 of the Equality Act 2010' leaves the HRA untouched.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan takes no action regarding the Human Rights Act. The obligation on public bodies under s.6 HRA to act compatibly with Convention rights is a separate and powerful legal requirement. A court could easily find that to avoid a breach of Article 14, a public authority must undertake a process of consideration very similar to a PSED assessment to ensure its actions are not discriminatory.

*What would close it:* Repeal or significant amendment of the Human Rights Act 1998, which is a far larger and more constitutionally significant undertaking than this proposal.

### 3. The repeal will be ineffective or create significant legal uncertainty in Scotland and Wales, where specific, devolved equality duties are built upon the foundation of Section 149.

**Mechanism:** `DISAPPLICATION`

**Rests on:** The Scotland Act 1998 and the Government of Wales Act 2006, which establish the legislative competence of the devolved administrations. The specific equality duties for Scotland and Wales are made under devolved powers.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The proposal is for a UK-wide Bill to repeal a section of a UK-wide Act, but the 'plan' and 'actions' make no mention of the devolved settlement or the need for Legislative Consent Motions.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan completely ignores the devolved context. Repealing s149 without the consent of the Scottish Parliament and Senedd Cymru would be politically explosive and legally complex. The devolved administrations could refuse consent, legislate to preserve the duties for devolved bodies, or argue that the repeal so fundamentally alters the 2010 Act that it cannot apply as intended to their existing specific duties, leading to prolonged litigation and administrative chaos.

*What would close it:* The plan would need to include a comprehensive strategy for engaging with devolved administrations and securing Legislative Consent Motions, or explicitly limit the repeal to England-only public bodies, which would undermine the stated goal.

### 4. The UK's international treaty obligations will be used to argue that a duty to proactively consider equality impacts remains a requirement of domestic law.

**Mechanism:** `ENTRENCHED_ELSEWHERE`

**Rests on:** International treaties ratified by the UK, such as the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW), which contain obligations to proactively advance equality.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan's focus is purely domestic, aiming to 'redirect public administration's focus back to service outcomes and ministerial accountability' without reference to international legal standards.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan does not address international law. While unincorporated treaties are not directly enforceable, courts use them as an aid to interpretation, especially when construing ambiguous statutes or developing the common law. In the vacuum left by s149's repeal, courts will be strongly invited to interpret remaining domestic obligations (under the HRA or common law) in a way that is compatible with the UK's international commitments, effectively re-importing a duty of proactive consideration.

*What would close it:* The Bill would need to contain a clause directing courts to disregard specific international treaty obligations when interpreting domestic law, a constitutionally novel and highly provocative step. Alternatively, the UK would have to withdraw from the relevant treaties.
