### 1. The 'legally authoritative guidance' is not legally authoritative and will be set aside by a court.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** The doctrine of parliamentary sovereignty and the separation of powers. Definitive interpretation of statute is a matter for the courts, not executive guidance. Established case law (e.g., R v Secretary of State for the Home Department, ex p Bracking [1999]; R (on the application of Marouf) v Secretary of State for the Home Department [2023]) has already established a wide, substantive meaning for 'due regard'.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The Cabinet Office... should issue revised, legally authoritative guidance on the interpretation and application of Section 149...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan's central action is to issue guidance. Guidance cannot override a statute or the body of case law interpreting it. A court adjudicating a challenge to a decision made under this new guidance would apply the statute and precedent, not the guidance. The plan contains no mechanism to make the guidance binding on a court.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation to amend Section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 itself.

### 2. The other limbs of the Public Sector Equality Duty, which justify proactive DEI, survive untouched and legally binding.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** Section 149(1)(b) ('advance equality of opportunity') and (c) ('foster good relations') of the Equality Act 2010, and the explanatory provision in s.149(3). These are concurrent duties, not subordinate to the duty to eliminate discrimination in s.149(1)(a).

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> This guidance must explicitly state that 'due regard' primarily means avoiding unlawful discrimination...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The guidance attempts to create a hierarchy between the three statutory duties, prioritising limb (a). This is an unlawful reading of the Act. A public body's lawyers will advise that they remain bound by all three duties. Proactive DEI initiatives (mentoring, staff networks, positive action) can be robustly defended as necessary to 'advance equality of opportunity' or 'foster good relations', irrespective of the guidance's focus on 'avoiding discrimination'.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation to amend Section 149 to either remove limbs (b) and (c) or explicitly subordinate them to limb (a).

### 3. The Civil Service's own published strategies provide an alternative, non-statutory source of authority for DEI work.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** The Civil Service Diversity and Inclusion Strategy 2022-2025, which includes the ambition to be the 'UK's most inclusive employer' and to 'go further than the current Equality Act provisions'.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The entire proposal focuses on the PSED as the sole driver of DEI, ignoring the Civil Service's independent adoption of these goals as a matter of policy.

**◐ partly closed** — The plan attacks the perceived legal compulsion but fails to explicitly rescind the independent policy commitment. While Treasury action on funding makes implementation harder, it does not remove the policy mandate. Departments can still justify DEI work as fulfilling the Civil Service's own published strategy, which remains in force.

*What would close it:* An explicit action to formally withdraw the Civil Service Diversity and Inclusion Strategy 2022-2025 and direct departments to remove corresponding objectives from their plans.

### 4. The plan makes the government's legal position worse by dismantling a predictable compliance framework, exposing it to less structured and more successful legal challenges.

**Mechanism:** `OTHER` — This route argues that the proposal, in trying to solve one problem, creates a more serious and less manageable legal vulnerability elsewhere in the same statutory scheme.

**Rests on:** The Equality Act 2010, particularly provisions on indirect discrimination (s.19) and positive action (s.158 & s.159). Also, the common law duty of public bodies to act rationally.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The approach is to force a distinction between mandatory legal compliance and discretionary policy...

**⚠⚠ **the plan makes it worse**** — The current DEI framework, while expansive, provides a structured way to demonstrate 'due regard' and gather data. By dismantling this and discouraging proactive measures, the plan makes it harder for the Civil Service to defend itself against claims of indirect discrimination. For example, if data collection on diversity in recruitment is scaled back, the Civil Service may be unable to show that a new 'merit-based' process does not have a discriminatory impact on a protected group. The plan removes the shield but not the sword.
