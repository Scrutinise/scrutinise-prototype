### 1. The effect of the Gender Recognition Act 2004 survives any change to the Equality Act 2010, because the proposal fails to amend the GRA itself. A court will read the two Acts together, preserving the 'for all purposes' effect of a GRC.

**Mechanism:** `ENTRENCHED_ELSEWHERE`

**Rests on:** Gender Recognition Act 2004, section 9(1), and the doctrine against implied repeal.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The 'ACTIONS' list only mentions an 'amendment to the Equality Act 2010'. It does not propose any amendment to the Gender Recognition Act 2004.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan, whether via guidance or primary legislation, only targets the Equality Act. It leaves the powerful 'for all purposes' provision in s.9(1) of the GRA untouched. A court would seek to reconcile the two statutes, likely by treating the GRC as a specific exception to the general rule in the amended EA, thereby defeating the proposal's objective.

*What would close it:* The amending legislation would need to state explicitly that the new definition of 'sex' applies 'notwithstanding section 9(1) of the Gender Recognition Act 2004'.

### 2. The statutory guidance will be struck down as ultra vires because it purports to resolve a conflict between two Acts of Parliament (the GRA 2004 and EA 2010), which is a function reserved for Parliament itself or the highest courts. A Minister cannot use guidance to effectively amend or repeal primary legislation.

**Mechanism:** `PROCEDURAL_SURVIVAL`

**Rests on:** The constitutional principle of Parliamentary sovereignty and the doctrine of ultra vires.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan is to 'use the power under the Equality Act 2010 to issue statutory guidance clarifying that 'sex' means biological sex for the purposes of the Act'.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan mistakes the nature of statutory guidance. Guidance can clarify ambiguity within an Act, but it cannot settle a direct conflict between two Acts. By attempting to nullify the effect of the GRA 2004 on the EA 2010, the guidance would exceed the Minister's powers and be quashed on judicial review. The procedural route of judicial review survives and would defeat the guidance.

*What would close it:* Abandoning the guidance route in favour of new primary legislation that explicitly amends both the Equality Act 2010 and the Gender Recognition Act 2004.

### 3. By removing the statutory protection of 'sex' under the Equality Act for GRC holders, the proposal forces them to rely on their less specific but potentially more powerful rights to privacy and family life under the Human Rights Act 1998.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights, as incorporated by the Human Rights Act 1998, and the jurisprudence of the European Court of Human Rights (e.g., Goodwin v UK).

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The proposal's diagnosis is that the Supreme Court ruling means 'obtaining a GRC does not change legal sex for the purposes of the Act'.

**⚠⚠ **the plan makes it worse**** — The proposal strips away a specific, qualified statutory protection (under the EA 2010) and replaces it with a general, fundamental right (under the HRA 1998). A court applying an Article 8 analysis would be less constrained by the specific exceptions in the Equality Act and would conduct a more free-ranging proportionality assessment, which could lead to outcomes less predictable and favourable to the proposer's client than the status quo.

*What would close it:* The plan would need to include provisions to disapply or modify the Human Rights Act 1998 in this context, which would be exceptionally difficult and controversial.

### 4. The Supreme Court's judgment in *For Women Scotland* will be read down to apply only to the specific context of single-sex service exceptions, not as a wholesale redefinition of 'sex' for all purposes of the Equality Act.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** The principle of statutory interpretation that courts avoid impliedly repealing one statute (GRA 2004) via the interpretation of another (EA 2010), and will prefer the narrowest possible ruling to resolve a case.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The entire diagnosis rests on the claim that the ruling 'has resolved the legal ambiguity, confirming 'sex' in the Equality Act 2010 means biological sex'.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan's reliance on statutory guidance is entirely dependent on a broad interpretation of the judgment. Any future court, when asked to judicially review the guidance, is highly likely to adopt a narrower reading of the Supreme Court's precedent, finding that it only clarified the operation of the existing exceptions in the EA 2010. This would remove the legal foundation for the guidance, causing it to fail.

*What would close it:* The plan would need to be based on new primary legislation rather than a contestable interpretation of case law.
