### 1. The statutory test for overriding the Bank's independence will be read so narrowly as to be unusable for the proposer's purpose.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** The principle of statutory interpretation that exceptional powers are construed narrowly, supported by the legislative history of the Bank of England Act 1998 (specifically, ministerial statements that the power would be used 'rarely, if at all').

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> Utilise the existing reserve power of the Treasury under Section 19 of the Bank of England Act 1998 to direct monetary policy in 'extreme economic circumstances'...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan focuses on building a political case based on the distributional consequences of monetary policy. This does not change the legal test in Section 19. A court would not accept that adverse distributional effects, even if severe, meet the threshold of 'extreme economic circumstances', which implies a systemic crisis or near-collapse of the economy.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation amending Section 19 of the 1998 Act to replace 'extreme economic circumstances' with a lower and more political threshold, such as 'to ensure coordination with fiscal policy'.

### 2. Any attempt by the Treasury to use its Section 19 reserve power will be challenged and quashed in court.

**Mechanism:** `PROCEDURAL_SURVIVAL`

**Rests on:** The common law of judicial review, specifically on grounds of illegality (misinterpreting the scope of the power) and irrationality (no reasonable authority could conclude the circumstances were 'extreme').

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> THE APPROACH: Utilise the existing reserve power of the Treasury under Section 19 of the Bank of England Act 1998 to direct monetary policy...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan to commission an audit and hold a debate builds a political justification, but it does not insulate the eventual decision from judicial review. The courts' power to review the exercise of statutory powers is a fundamental constitutional principle. The plan does nothing to oust the court's jurisdiction.

*What would close it:* New primary legislation containing a clear and explicit ouster clause preventing judicial review of a direction made under Section 19. Such clauses are constitutionally controversial and often read down by the courts.

### 3. Repealing the statutory framework for independence would subject the government's direct monetary policy decisions to intrusive common law judicial review.

**Mechanism:** `OTHER` — Creation of a reviewable power at common law upon repeal of the statutory scheme.

**Rests on:** The common law of judicial review. In the absence of the 1998 Act's framework, any monetary policy decision made directly by ministers would be a reviewable administrative act, open to challenge on broad grounds such as improper purpose, irrationality, or procedural fairness.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> ACTIONS: Draft a Bill to amend or repeal the relevant sections of the Bank of England Act 1998 that confer operational independence...

**⚠⚠ **the plan makes it worse**** — The plan to repeal the Act is the direct cause of this problem. It removes the current settlement, where the MPC's expert judgment is given a wide margin of deference by courts, and replaces it with a vacuum. The common law would fill that vacuum, making ministers directly accountable in court for complex economic decisions, a far more volatile and legally perilous situation than the status quo.

### 4. The Bank's independence will be defended as a constitutional principle that cannot be overridden by a simple statutory power.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** An argument based on common law constitutionalism, asserting that the long-standing, cross-party settlement on Bank independence has hardened into a constitutional convention that the courts will protect from executive encroachment, citing the principles in cases like R (Miller) v The Prime Minister.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> THE DIAGNOSIS: The pivotal obstacle to reversing this is not legal but political: a deeply entrenched, cross-party consensus...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan correctly identifies the political consensus but mistakes it for a purely political obstacle. It fails to recognise that this 25-year consensus may have acquired legal or constitutional force. The plan's actions (audit, debate) are aimed at breaking the political consensus, but this does not address the argument that the consensus has already created a constitutional reality the courts might enforce.

*What would close it:* Clear and express primary legislation ('clear statutory words') that not only repeals the independence provisions but also explicitly addresses and overrides this constitutional convention.
