### 1. The 'legally authoritative guidance' from the Cabinet Office will be read by the courts as subordinate to, and incapable of overriding, the substantive interpretation of the Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED) established in primary legislation and binding case law.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** Section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 and the body of case law interpreting 'due regard' as a substantive, rigorous duty (e.g., R (on the application of Marouf) v Secretary of State for the Home Department).

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan's entire strategy rests on the issuance of 'revised, legally authoritative guidance'.

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan's central tool is executive guidance, which is legally insufficient to alter the meaning of a statutory duty as interpreted by the courts. A court will simply rule that any public body following this guidance to the exclusion of its full statutory obligations under s.149 is acting unlawfully. The plan does not amend the primary legislation, so the underlying duty remains unchanged.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation to amend Section 149 of the Equality Act 2010.

### 2. The proposal's attempt to prioritise the first aim of the PSED (eliminating discrimination) over the other two (advancing equality of opportunity, fostering good relations) will be challenged as an unlawful attempt to fetter the discretion of public bodies, who are required to have due regard to all three statutory aims.

**Mechanism:** `NARROW_READING`

**Rests on:** The plain text of Section 149(1) of the Equality Act 2010, which lists three co-equal aims without establishing a hierarchy.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The guidance must explicitly state that 'due regard' *primarily* means avoiding unlawful discrimination...

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan's own wording creates the legal vulnerability. By instructing that one part of the statutory duty is 'primary', the guidance misrepresents the law and directs public bodies to act on an incorrect legal basis. A court is likely to find the guidance itself unlawful for attempting to rewrite a statutory provision that gives no such primacy.

*What would close it:* Primary legislation amending s.149(1) to create a hierarchy of duties.

### 3. Many of the 'proactive DEI initiatives' the plan seeks to eliminate will continue, justified not under the PSED, but under the specific statutory permissions for positive action in the Equality Act 2010, which the plan fails to address.

**Mechanism:** `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCE`

**Rests on:** Sections 158 (General positive action) and 159 (Positive action in recruitment and promotion) of the Equality Act 2010.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The plan's diagnosis and actions are focused exclusively on narrowing the interpretation of the Public Sector Equality Duty (Section 149).

**⚠ **the plan does NOT close it**** — The plan misdiagnoses the problem by assuming all DEI initiatives flow from an expansive interpretation of the PSED. It completely overlooks the separate, explicit statutory gateways for positive action. Departments can continue targeted outreach, mentoring, and even use tie-break provisions by re-characterising them as lawful positive action under ss. 158-159, rendering the new PSED guidance irrelevant to these activities.

*What would close it:* The repeal or amendment of Sections 158 and 159 of the Equality Act 2010.

### 4. Replacing a settled body of practice with legally uncertain guidance will expose every relevant recruitment and promotion decision to judicial review, creating significant litigation risk and administrative paralysis. This makes the cure worse than the disease.

**Mechanism:** `OTHER` — Creation of legal uncertainty and litigation risk.

**Rests on:** The principle of legal certainty and the high risk of judicial review challenges to decisions made under the new, legally contentious guidance.

**The opening, in the proposal's own words:**

> The entire plan is to replace the current interpretation with a new, 'narrowed' one via guidance.

**⚠⚠ **the plan makes it worse**** — The proposal moves the Civil Service from a position of legal stability (even if the outcomes are disliked by the proposer) to one of profound legal uncertainty. Every decision made in reliance on the new guidance will be vulnerable to challenge on the grounds that the decision-maker failed to comply with the *actual* PSED. This will invite litigation, delay decision-making, and likely lead to the guidance being struck down, creating more chaos than the status quo.
