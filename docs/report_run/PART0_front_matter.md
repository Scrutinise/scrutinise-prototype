# What this is — in more detail

⚠ **The programme is twelve measures, not one.** This pass works three of them and registers the
rest.

## Across the whole programme

- **The register** — every change the thesis calls for, in the proposer's own words, with our best
  guess at the legislation that would have to change, and why we think so
- **The thesis stated back** — the argument in disciplined form, so that any misreading surfaces
  here rather than sixty pages later
- **Four mechanisms, one problem** — four different remedies proposed within a year by people on the
  same side of the argument. Where they diverge, and what turns on which is right
- **The absorption question** — whether repeal alone achieves what is intended, tested against the
  authorities and against two published legal analyses
- **The devolution wall** — what repeal does to three devolution statutes, and what refused consent
  would mean
- **Who else has said what** — the published positions on each measure, including the ones that cut
  against the thesis and the ones that have overtaken it
- **The questions you must answer** — with options, and what each rules out

## For each measure worked

- **The law as it stands** — what the target Act does and does not do
- **Collision report** — the other legislation that refers to it, counted and grouped by pattern
- **Common law** — the judgments, and how the courts have read the statute
- **What was tried before** — earlier attempts, and what happened to them
- **How hard this will be** — the barriers, the likely opposition, what is most likely to go wrong
- **Who will support and who will oppose** — people and groups with a stated view

- **The published record, triaged** — every source named by four independent research passes, each
  marked verified, unverified, or invented. ⚠ *Some of them were invented, and that is itself one of
  the findings*

## Not in this pass, and named rather than omitted

- **Northern Ireland and the Convention** — the competing expert positions on the Belfast/Good Friday
  Agreement, located but not yet set out from their own texts. *(The Northern Ireland position on the
  Equality Act is worked, in §4.9.)*
- **The interlock graph** — whether the measures must move together, as the thesis asserts
- **The remaining nine measures** at any depth beyond the register
- **Cost-benefit and regulatory burden**
- **Where the research changed our mind** — recorded as it happened, not yet written up

Each finding supported by Scrutinise's research against the statute book, parliamentary debate,
committee reports and case law.

## What this is not

A finished proposal, a recommendation, or a costing. **Nothing here argues that this programme
should proceed.** It sets out what proceeding would involve.

It is also not complete, and does not pretend to be. It is a first pass whose purpose is to show the
shape and depth of what the programme entails. Carrying it through would need planning, strategy and
resourcing well beyond anything in this document.

**And it is a pilot.** The instrument is new and this is among the first proposals put through it.
Where a section misses, that is information we want: some items will be a simple deletion, others
will open an avenue that has to be worked properly. **Telling us which is which is the most useful
thing a reader can do.**

## Where the answers came from

Every finding traces to a source and is dated. Where Scrutinise is reasoning rather than citing, it
says so. Where the record is silent, it says that too — **a gap that names itself is worth more than
one that looks like an absence of evidence.**

⚠ **Three limits you should know before you read anything else.**

**Our case law begins in 2003.** Not by choice: the free public record starts in 2001 at the
earliest, The National Archives has confirmed the pre-2001 paper record will not be digitised, and
the main free database does not permit bulk access. Authorities older than that are cited from
public sources and marked as such. We have no citator, so we cannot confirm that none has since been
doubted — a check we recommend before the argument is relied on.

**Quotations from spoken material are machine-transcribed.** Where two independent transcription
engines agree, the text is treated as sound. Where only one exists, the passage is either verified by
ear or paraphrased and labelled as a paraphrase. It is never quoted on a single machine source.

**Counts are counts of what we hold.** Every number states what it counts and out of what. None is
presented as complete.
