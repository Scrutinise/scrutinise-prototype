## Item 2 — the live correction. **CCW's §4.4 is right.**

This is the one the brief flagged, so it is answered first and explicitly.

**GoWA 2006 s.108A(2)(e) — LIVE, and it is the Welsh equivalent of Scotland Act s.29(2)(d):**

> **108A(1)** An Act of the Senedd is not law so far as any provision of the Act is outside the
> Senedd's legislative competence.
> **(2)** A provision is outside that competence so far as any of the following apply— …
> **(e) it is incompatible with the Convention rights** ….

**Both Prosperity Institute citations fail:**

- **GoWA 1998 s.94 — REPEALED.** The revised text is the CLML omission marker in full:
  `94 . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .` There is nothing there to amend.
- **GoWA 2006 s.80 — live, but off-point.** It reads: *"An assimilated obligation of the United
  Kingdom is also an obligation of the Welsh Ministers if and to the extent that the obligation
  could be implemented … by the exercise by the Welsh Ministers of any of their functions."* That is
  about **assimilated (retained EU) obligations**, not Convention rights. The phrase "Convention
  rights" does not appear in it.

⚠ Also note **GoWA 2006 s.108** — the predecessor competence section — is likewise repealed. Anyone
working from an older text will reach for it.

**So: do not change §4.4.** s.108A(2)(e) is correct, in force, and the exact structural parallel.

## Item 4, and a finding that goes wider than the item

**Scotland Act 1998 s.126(1) — confirmed:**

> **"the Convention rights"** has the same meaning as in the Human Rights Act 1998

The definitional gap in §4.4 holds. And because the same question arises for the other two
settlements, all three were checked rather than one:

| Act | interpretation provision | definition |
|---|---|---|
| Scotland Act 1998 | s.126(1) | *"the Convention rights" has the same meaning as in the Human Rights Act 1998* |
| Northern Ireland Act 1998 | s.98(1) | *"the Convention rights" has the same meaning as in the Human Rights Act 1998* |
| Government of Wales Act 2006 | s.158(1) | *"the Convention rights" has the same meaning as in the Human Rights Act 1998 (c. 42)* |

**All three route through the Human Rights Act.** Repeal it without consequential amendment and
three competence tests point at a definition that is no longer there.

### ⚠ But Northern Ireland has a second ECHR hook that does *not*

Checking Schedule 2 rather than assuming it was off-point produced the opposite of what I expected.
**Sch 2 para 3** makes an excepted matter of:

> observing and implementing international obligations and obligations under **the Human Rights
> Convention**.
> In this paragraph **"the Human Rights Convention"** means … (a) the Convention for the Protection
> of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms, agreed by the Council of Europe at Rome on 4th November
> 1950; and (b) any Protocols to that Convention which have been ratified by the United Kingdom.

That definition **names the treaty directly and does not route through the Human Rights Act.** It is
therefore not exposed to the definitional gap that ss.6(2)(c) and 24(1)(a) are. Whether that changes
the disposition is CCW's call — but the NI settlement is not uniform with the other two, and a
consequential-amendments schedule drafted from the Scottish model would miss this.

## Item 1 — NIA 1998 ss.6, 24, Sch 2

All three live. The Convention constraints are in the subsections, not the section headings:

- **s.6(2)(c)** — *"it is incompatible with any of the Convention rights"* — binds **Acts of the Assembly**.
- **s.24(1)(a)** — *"is incompatible with any of the Convention rights"* — binds **Ministers and departments**: *"A Minister or Northern Ireland department has no power to make, confirm or approve any subordinate legislation, or to do any act, so far as the legislation or act …"*
- **Sch 2** — see above. It carries an ECHR constraint of a **different kind** (an excepted matter with its own definition), so it belongs in the citation.

⚠ I had provisionally marked Sch 2 **wrong** on the grounds that it is the "excepted matters"
schedule and the phrase "incompatible with" does not appear in it (confirmed: it does not). Reading
it before publishing that verdict showed four occurrences of "Convention" and a self-contained ECHR
definition. **The verdict is correct, not wrong.**

## Item 3 — s.57(2) does add something

- **s.29(2)(d)** — *"it is incompatible with any of the Convention rights …"* — a limit on the
  **legislative competence of the Parliament**; it makes an Act of the Scottish Parliament *not law*.
- **s.57(2)** — *"A member of the Scottish Government has no power to make any subordinate
  legislation, or to do any other act, so far as the legislation or act is incompatible with any of
  the Convention rights …"*

**Answer to the brief's question: yes.** s.29 governs primary legislation of the Parliament; s.57(2)
governs **acts and subordinate legislation of the Scottish Government**. Removing only s.29(2)(d)
would leave Scottish Ministers still bound. The Prosperity Institute is right to cite both.

⚠ **s.57(1) is repealed** (omission marker), so anyone quoting "s.57" as a whole is quoting a
section with a hole in it.

## Item 6 — the TCA. **Reachable, and both articles confirmed.**

Held in `uk-treaties`: *UK/EU and EAEC: Trade and Cooperation Agreement*, **444,778 words**,
availability `full`
([source](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/608ae0c0d3bf7f0136332887/TS_8.2021_UK_EU_EAEC_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement.pdf)).

> **ARTICLE 524 — Protection of human rights and fundamental freedoms**
> 1. The cooperation provided for in this Part is based on the Parties' and Member States'
> long-standing respect for democracy, the rule of law and the protection of fundamental rights and
> freedoms of individuals, including as set out in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and in
> the European Convention on Human Rights, and on the importance of giving effect to the rights and
> freedoms in that Convention domestically.
> 2. Nothing in this Part modifies the obligation to respect fundamental rights and legal principles
> as reflected, in particular, in the European Convention on Human Rights …

> **ARTICLE 692 — Termination**
> 1. Without prejudice to Article 779, each Party may at any moment terminate this Part by written
> notification through diplomatic channels. In that event, this Part shall cease to be in force on
> the first day of the ninth month following the date of notification.
> 2. **However, if this Part is terminated on account of the United Kingdom or a Member State having
> denounced the European Convention on Human Rights or Protocols 1, 6 or 13 thereto**, this Part
> shall cease to be in force as of the date that such denunciation becomes effective …

**The Prosperity Institute's citation is correct on every element**: the article numbers, that 692 is
the termination trigger, and that it extends to Protocols 1, 6 and 13.

⚠ **One scope point they do not state.** Article 692 terminates **"this Part"** — Part Three, law
enforcement and judicial cooperation — not the whole Agreement.

### ⚠ How this was nearly reported as unverified

The brief says the TCA "is not in the legislation corpus — if you cannot reach it, say so rather
than approximating". That is true of the CLML, which holds UK legislation and not treaties, and my
first query scoped the search to `corpus IN ('eur-lex','retained-eu')` and returned **0** — which
read as confirmation of the brief's own expectation. Widening the scope returned **644** matching
titles and, among them, the full agreement. **A search scoped narrower than the thing it is looking
for will confirm any absence you please**, and it is worse when the expected answer is the one it
confirms.

## Item 7 — the provisions are right; the Act is misnamed

The citation reads **"EU (Withdrawal Agreement) Act 2018 ss.7A and 8C"**. There is no such Act. Both
sections are live, and both are in the **European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018** (`ukpga/2018/16`):

> **7A(1)** Subsection (2) applies to— (a) all such rights, powers, liabilities, obligations and
> restrictions from time to time created or arising by or under the withdrawal agreement … as in
> accordance with the withdrawal agreement are **without further enactment** to be given legal effect
> or used in the United Kingdom.

> **8C(1)** A Minister of the Crown may by regulations make such provision as the Minister considers
> appropriate— (a) to implement the **Protocol on Ireland/Northern Ireland** in the withdrawal
> agreement, (b) to supplement the effect of section 7A in relation to the Protocol …

They were inserted by the **European Union (Withdrawal *Agreement*) Act 2020** (`ukpga/2020/1`) —
confirmed from the inserting provisions themselves:

- **EUWAA 2020 s.5**: *"After section 7 of the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 (status of retained EU law) insert— … 7A …"*
- **EUWAA 2020 s.21**: *"After section 8B of the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 … insert— … 8C …"*

**So the citation conflates two Acts and gets the year of the second one wrong.** The correct form is
*European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 ss.7A and 8C, as inserted by the European Union (Withdrawal
Agreement) Act 2020 ss.5 and 21.* The substance the PI relies on is sound; only the label is wrong —
but in a report about statutes it is the kind of wrong that costs a reader's confidence.

## The REUL sunset — what actually happened

**What the Act as enacted does.** s.1 of the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023:

> **1(1)** Legislation **listed in Schedule 1** is revoked at the end of 2023, to the extent
> specified there.
> **(2)** In that Schedule— (a) Part 1 lists subordinate legislation; (b) Part 2 lists retained
> direct EU legislation.
> **(3)** The revocation of an instrument … does not affect an amendment made by the instrument …
> **(4)** Subsection (1) does not apply to anything specified in regulations made by a relevant
> national authority.
> **(5)** No regulations may be made under subsection (4) after 31 October 2023.

**Schedule 1 is a named list** — 136,432 characters of it. The Bill as introduced carried a **general
sunset** on retained EU law with specified exceptions; **the Act as enacted reverses the default**.
Instead of everything falling unless saved, only what is listed falls.

**Why it changed — from Hansard, not commentary.** The corpus holds the exchange. Kemi Badenoch,
Secretary of State for Business and Trade, in the House on **10 May 2023**
([TheyWorkForYou source](https://www.theyworkforyou.com/pwdata/scrapedxml/debates/debates2023-05-11a.xml)):

> yesterday we tabled an amendment to the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Bill that **amends
> the operation of the sunset in clause 1**. It is a technical change that introduces to the Bill a
> schedule of retained EU law that will be revoked on 31 December 2023.

> over the past year, as Whitehall Departments have been working hard to identify retained EU law to
> preserve, reform or revoke, **it has become clear that time constraints have led to the programme
> becoming more about preserving EU laws than prioritising meaningful reform**. That is why we are
> proposing a new approach.

> **We could have ended up with a programme of 450 statutory instruments to preserve EU law.**

> My hon. Friend claims that this is a change of policy, but it is **a change of approach**.

> We tabled the amendment **in response to concerns raised in this House**.

⚠ **These are the minister's stated reasons, quoted.** Whether they are the operative reasons, and
what they imply for the viability of a sunset-clause model in the Prosperity Institute's Case Law
Review Commission, is CCW's call. The retrieval establishes three facts: the sunset **was** changed
before Royal Assent; the change **reversed the default** from revoke-unless-saved to revoke-if-listed;
and the reason the responsible minister gave on the floor was **delivery capacity within the time
available**.
