# Part 6 · The questions

## 6 · The questions only David can answer

**This is what the document is for.** Everything before it exists to make these questions precise. None can be answered from the record; all of them change what gets drafted.

**They are ordered by what they block.** The first five stop other work until they are answered.

---

### The five that block everything else

**1. Which of the four plans is intended?**

Annulment, the Case Law Review Commission, structured withdrawal, or leave-and-re-enact. **They are not variants of one plan.** They produce different Bills, different timetables and different residual rights. *Recommendation: answer this before any drafting begins.*

**2. Is the replacement Convention-shaped, or deliberately not?**

Lord Wolfson warns against a Bill of Rights modelled on the Convention. Lord Sumption proposes exactly that. **The two most senior legal voices on the same side disagree about the single most consequential drafting decision in the programme.**

**3. Is the target the statute, or the case law decided under it?**

These need different instruments, and **only the second reaches the absorption problem David has correctly identified.** Annulment reaches statutes. The problem lives in judgments.

**4. On the Equality Act: the whole Act, or section 149 and the duties?**

The Bill now before the Commons takes the second. The official Opposition has taken the second. We read David as taking the first. **Those are different Bills with different consequential tails**, and the difference is now a live political question rather than a drafting nicety.

**5. Is section 75 of the Northern Ireland Act 1998 a target?**

The list does not say. **If the objective is UK-wide, it must be** — because the public sector equality duty has never applied in Northern Ireland and repealing it changes nothing there. If it is not a target, the Equality Act measure applies to Great Britain only and should say so. *Recommendation: state the answer explicitly in the next version of the list, either way.*

---

### On the programme's structure

**6. Are the Equality Act, DEI and gender self-identification accepted as one workstream?** *Recommendation: yes. It removes two Bills and loses nothing.*

**7. Is the omnibus Act still required if the interlock runs through the case law rather than the statutes?** *Recommendation: this is the most valuable question in the list. An omnibus Bill justified by statutory interdependence is vulnerable to the objection that the statutes are separable. One justified by common-law interdependence is not — and the evidence supports the second.*

**8. Which Acts are in scope of "1997 to 2010"?** The measure is currently defined by a date range. That is the first thing a drafter would ask.

**9. Given that the one exclusion clause the courts upheld was narrow and surgical, is a broad annulment device still preferred?**

---

### On individual measures

**10. On the civil service: is the target the 2010 Act or the 1854 settlement?** They are different targets requiring different instruments, and repealing the first leaves the second standing.

**11. What does Lord Maude know that makes him stop short of repeal?** He is not a defender of the machine, and he reached the same diagnosis.

**12. What is the answer to the objection that institutional change cannot fix problems that are political in origin?** This is the strongest available criticism of the programme's whole method and it deserves an answer at its strongest.

**13. On the Bank of England: is the objective repeal, or legitimacy?** The evidence reads the complaint as being about a political question reclassified as technical. If that is right, repeal is not obviously the instrument.

**14. On charities: a blanket restriction, or accountability for the grant?** David names no instrument. The two produce entirely different Bills. *Recommendation: the second is narrower, faster and needs no legislation.*

**15. Is the Sentencing Council one of the twelve, or an example of the quango measure?**

**16. Given *For Women Scotland*, how much of the gender self-identification objection survives — and is legislation still the instrument for what remains?**

**17. Does the programme accept or reject the Maude Review's diagnosis of civil service accountability?**

---

### On what is already happening

**18. Wood's Bill takes withdrawal without repealing the Human Rights Act. Morrissey's takes section 149 without the rest of the Act. Are these acceptable narrower versions, or do they miss the point?** *Recommendation: answer this one early. It is the difference between joining a live legislative effort and starting a separate one.*

**19. The Public Office (Accountability) Bill is moving in the opposite direction with government time behind it. Does the programme oppose it, ignore it, or absorb it?**

**20. Does the Chișinău Declaration disturb the premise that the Convention system cannot be reformed?** In May 2026 all 46 Council of Europe states adopted a political declaration on the Convention and migration. It is not binding and does not bind the Strasbourg court — but 46 states have just done, by declaration, something the premise says cannot be done.

---

### On the four unsourced measures

**21. Are the Lord Chancellor, the Climate Change Act, the Judicial Appointments Commission and the removal of judges part of the programme?** We could not find them in 287 transcripts. **They are being attributed to David in public.** *Recommendation: confirm or disown each one explicitly — silence lets the attribution stand.*

---

### On delivery

**22. Is the mass creation of peers still necessary?** The European Union (Notification of Withdrawal) Act 2017 went from introduction to Royal Assent in **seven weeks, through a hostile House of Lords, without a single peer being created** — on the strength of a manifesto commitment and the Salisbury convention. **This is evidence that makes the programme easier.**

**23. What delay would the Parliament Acts impose if the Salisbury convention did not hold?** Not yet established.

**24. Which of Dominic Grieve's stated consequences — the Trade and Cooperation Agreement, Horizon, Northern Ireland — are accepted, and which are contested?**

**25. Is refused consent from Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland accepted as a cost to be paid, or is consent to be sought?** Lord Wolfson's advice does not address the devolution statutes at all. The nearest thing to an answer in it is a drafting instruction that a manifesto should say the UK will proceed regardless.

---

### And the question we most want answered

**26. Where have we read David wrongly?**

**That is the first task this document asks of its reader, and it is not a formality.** Every entry in the register is our reading of David's words. If an entry is wrong, everything built on it is wrong — and we would rather be told now than have it printed.

# Part 7 · How this was made

## 7 · How this was made, and what it cannot do yet

**This is the honesty section.** It is here because a document that documents other people's errors should document its own.

---

### What Scrutinise did

**Twelve separate runs of the instrument, one per measure**, each searching the statute book, the parliamentary record, committee reports and court judgments, and each drafting a diagnosis and a plan from what it found. **No run was told what any other run found.**

Across the twelve: **1,100 pieces of evidence, 937 of them with a full citation and a working link, and 497 open challenges raised against the drafts.** No pass failed.

Alongside that, a separate search of the whole statute book for every provision that points at the Acts in question — which is where the numbers in Part 4 come from.

---

### The rule everything is built on

**Every legal assertion has to resolve to an actual document. Where it cannot, we say so rather than fill the gap.**

That rule was tested harder than we intended.

⚠⚠ **The first run of the flagship measure went out with the search switched off.** Two settings were missing from the machine that runs the searches. Eighteen searches failed and returned nothing. **The run still completed, reported success, and produced eleven sections of drafted argument — with zero citations and zero links.**

**What it did not do is invent anything.** Given nothing, it recorded nothing: no sources, no findings, a note that the search had failed, and **fifteen stated gaps.** Its own summary line read *"Nothing retrieved produced a finding."* On the screen, the user was told plainly: *"The corpus search didn't complete, so there's nothing here yet. Nothing has been guessed or filled in — it just didn't run."*

**We are printing this because it is the strongest evidence in the document that the rule holds.** It happened by accident, under the worst conditions available, and the instrument told the truth about it.

⚠ **And the defect it exposed is worth naming precisely, because it is narrower than it first appears.** The failure was shown clearly to a person looking at the screen. It was *not* shown to an automated process, which saw only a completed run. **The product is honest to a human and silent to a script** — and we found that out by being the script.

**The run was repeated with the search working. That is the version in this document.**

---

### What we got wrong

**Five citations we were given turned out not to exist.** Set out in full in Part 2.7, with what is actually there instead.

**And one of ours.** While checking a technical setting we reasoned from how often something appeared in our own files rather than from what was actually running, and reached the right answer from the wrong direction. It was harmless this time. **It is the same error as reading a summary instead of the source, which is the error this whole document is about.**

**We also had a date and a direction wrong** on the Maude Review — 2024 rather than 2023, and treated as supporting the programme when it does the opposite. Corrected in Part 2.5, and recorded rather than quietly fixed.

**And we were about to print a claim about our own coverage that was false** — that our records were blind to Bills before Parliament. They are not. We checked before printing. The real limit is narrower and is stated in Part 2.6.

---

### What this document cannot do yet

**Our record of court judgments begins in 2003**, because the free public record does not exist earlier. The National Archives has confirmed the pre-2001 paper record will not be digitised, and the main free database will not permit bulk access. **We have no citator**, so we cannot confirm that no judgment cited here has since been doubted or overruled. That check takes a subscribing lawyer about ten minutes and should be done before this argument is relied on.

**Our record of Bills before Parliament stops at a point** — 6,574 records to 3 August 2026 — and the two Bills this report turns on are above it. They were found by hand.

⚠ **Six of the eleven measure runs were cut short by a spending limit** and asked six research questions instead of seven. **Some of what those runs record as gaps are questions never asked, not questions asked and unanswered — and the output does not distinguish the two.** No count in this document should be read as the limit of what the instrument can find.

**We have not run a cross-measure search.** Every run was asked about its own measure. Part 3's finding that the measures are separable is therefore evidence, not proof — nothing was hunting for the links it did not find.

**And this is a first pass.** Two measures are worked in full and ten are summarised. Carrying the programme through would need planning, strategy and resourcing far beyond anything here.

---

### What we would do next

1. **Check the register with David.** Nothing downstream is safe until the twelve entries are confirmed.
2. **A cross-measure search**, to test Part 3's separability finding properly rather than by inference.
3. **The remaining ten measures to the depth of Part 4.**
4. **A citator check** on every judgment cited.
5. **Bills before Parliament brought up to date**, and linked to the Acts they would amend — so the instrument can answer, for anyone, the question we had to answer by hand: *has somebody already put this in a Bill?*

---

### And what this is a pilot of

**Scrutinise takes a proposal and works out what delivering it would actually require.** This is among the first programmes put through it, and much the largest.

**Where a section misses, that is information we want.** Some items will be a simple deletion. Others will open something that has to be worked properly. **Telling us which is which is the most useful thing a reader of this document can do.**

*Scrutinise is free and non-partisan, and is available on identical terms to anyone.*
