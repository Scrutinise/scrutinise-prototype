# Part 3 — The interlock: does the programme have to move as one?

**Draft 1, 3 Sep 2026, CCW.** Built from twelve independent runs of the instrument, one per measure,
each blind to the other eleven. **No run was told what any other run found.**

---

## The claim being tested

The proposer's central structural assertion is that the twelve measures interlock — that undoing any
one of them leaves the others standing to reproduce the effect complained of, so a government
proceeding measure by measure will be defeated by whatever it has not yet reached. That is the
argument for one omnibus Act rather than a sequence of Bills, and **everything about the programme's
delivery follows from it.**

**Nobody has tested it.** This section does, twice — once against the statute book, once against the
instrument's own reasoning.

⚠ **What follows is a test, not a verdict.** Its limits are stated at the end and they are real.

---

## Test one: do the measures touch the same law?

Each of the twelve builds retrieved its own evidence independently. **If the measures interlock as
claimed, they should keep landing on the same Acts and the same provisions.**

**Across all twelve builds, 23 Acts of Parliament were cited. Nineteen were cited by exactly one
measure.**

### The four Acts that more than one measure touches

| Act | Measures | What the sharing is |
|---|---|---|
| **Equality Act 2010** | **M-02** Equality Act · **M-08** DEI · **M-09** gender self-ID | ⚠⚠ Three measures, one Act |
| **Equality Act 2006** | M-02 · M-08 | The Commission's founding Act |
| **Constitutional Reform Act 2005** | M-03 Supreme Court · M-12 Great Repeal | Expected — M-12 contains M-03 |
| **Human Rights Act 1998** | M-01 HRA · M-12 Great Repeal | Expected — M-12 contains M-01 |

### And at provision level, seven shared provisions in the entire programme

| Provision | Measures |
|---|---|
| Equality Act 2010, **s.149** | M-02 · M-08 |
| Equality Act 2010, **s.153** | M-02 · M-08 |
| Equality Act 2010, Sch. 18 para. 3 | M-02 · M-08 |
| Equality Act 2010, Sch. 26 para. 10 | M-02 · M-08 |
| Equality Act 2006, s.31 | M-02 · M-08 |
| Equality Act 2006, s.32 | M-02 · M-08 |
| **Human Rights Act 1998, s.21** | M-01 · M-12 |

⚠⚠ **Six of the seven are the same pair.** Strip out the two measures that are the programme
restating itself (M-12 contains M-01 and M-03 by construction), and **the entire cross-measure
overlap in the statute book is M-02, M-08 and M-09 sharing the Equality Act.**

---

## ⚠⚠ Finding one: three of the twelve measures are substantially one measure

**M-02 (repeal the Equality Act), M-08 (end DEI practice in the civil service) and M-09 (reverse
gender self-identification) converge on the same statute**, and M-02 and M-08 share six provisions
and eight court judgments between them.

**This was not designed in.** The three were built separately, from separate statements by the
proposer, on separate days of the corpus's reading. They converged because the law does.

**What it means in practice, and it is the most immediately useful thing in this section:**

- **M-08 has no separate legislative existence.** Its evidence is M-02's evidence. Drafting it as a
  distinct measure produces a Bill that either duplicates the Equality Act repeal or contradicts it.
- **M-09 is resolved by litigation, not legislation** — see §4.8 — and what remains of it sits inside
  the same Act.
- **So the Equality Act strand is one workstream with three faces, not three measures.**

⚠ **The programme is more accurately described as ten measures than twelve**, and saying so costs the
proposer nothing: it removes two Bills from a legislative programme whose principal difficulty is
parliamentary time.

---

## ⚠ Finding two: outside that cluster, the measures are markedly separable

**Nineteen of twenty-three Acts are touched by exactly one measure.** The Bank of England Act 1998
appears in M-07 and nowhere else. The Sentencing Council's founding provisions appear in M-11 and
nowhere else. The charities framework appears in M-10 and nowhere else.

**On this evidence, most of the programme could be legislated one measure at a time without the
remainder defeating it** — which is the opposite of the claim the omnibus Act rests on.

⚠⚠ **This is the weaker of the two findings and must be read with its limit attached.** Absence of
shared citations is not absence of dependency. Two measures can be deeply interdependent without
citing the same Act — through convention, through institutional practice, or through a mechanism the
retrieval never had reason to look for. **Each build was asked about its own measure and nothing was
looking for cross-links.** So this is evidence that the measures are *separable in the statute book*,
not proof that they are separable in politics.

**The positive finding is strong because convergence was not designed in. The negative finding is
suggestive because nothing was hunting for it.** Both are stated at their true weight.

---

## Test two: where the courts join measures the statute book does not

Ninety judgments were cited across the twelve builds. Seventeen appear in more than one measure, and
**the clusters do not follow the statutory ones.**

| Judgments shared | Measures | What the shared cases are about |
|---|---|---|
| 3 — *Lockhart*, *DPP v Haw*, *Castle v CPS* | **M-04** quangos · **M-06** civil service · **M-08** DEI | Public authority conduct and the limits of official action |
| 8 | M-02 · M-08 | The Equality Act cluster again |
| 4 — incl. *R v Adams (NI)*, *Austin v Chief Constable of Surrey* | M-04 · M-06 | Delegated decision-making and lawful authority |
| *For Women Scotland* | **M-03** Supreme Court · **M-09** gender self-ID | The Court doing the thing the programme wants done |
| *Buddington* | M-03 · M-07 | — |

⚠ **The M-04 / M-06 / M-08 case-law cluster is a genuine interlock the statute book does not show.**
Quangos, the civil service and DEI practice share no Act, but they share judgments about what public
authorities may lawfully do and who answers for it. **That is the accountability question underneath
all three**, and it is the strongest evidence in this section *for* the proposer's structural claim —
found by the courts rather than by the drafters.

**So the honest answer to "do they interlock" is: not through the statutes, and partly through the
case law** — which is itself an argument for the absorption thesis in §4.2, because it is the same
observation: what binds these measures together lives in the judgments, not in the Acts.

---

## ⚠⚠ Test three: what the instrument said when it disagreed

The twelve builds were not asked to agree. On **four of twelve measures the instrument proposed a
different remedy from the proposer's**, in its own drafted guiding policy, having read the record.

**And these must be split into two classes, because the report's own rule requires it.**

### (a) Where the instrument reached it on material we did not supply

| | Measure | The proposer's remedy | What the instrument proposed instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M-07** | Bank of England | Reverse operational independence | Diagnosed the 1998 Act as having *"reclassified the inherently political and distributional choices"* of monetary policy as technical ones — and proposed attacking the **legitimacy of the consensus** by reframing it as a question of democratic accountability, rather than repealing the Act |
| **M-10** | Charities | Stop publicly funded charities campaigning | *"Fix accountability for the funding decision with the grantor, not by creating a new blanket ban"* |

⚠ **Neither was seeded.** For M-07 we supplied only the Act that conferred independence and a note
that the measure appears once in the corpus. For M-10 we supplied the *available* instruments and
recorded that the proposer names none — **the choice between them is the instrument's own.**

**M-10 is the sharper of the two.** Given a measure with no stated instrument, it did not invent one
for him; it identified where the accountability actually sits and proposed the narrowest thing that
would reach it.

### (b) Where we supplied the counter-material and it built on it

| | Measure | What it did with it |
|---|---|---|
| **M-06** | Civil service | *"The proposer misidentifies the target. The obstacle is not the Constitutional Reform and Governance Act…"* — and then proposed forcing *"a direct confrontation with the actual target"* rather than *"pursuing a legally ineffective"* repeal |
| **M-09** | Gender self-ID | Opened on *For Women Scotland* having *"resolved"* the question, and proposed **using existing powers to enforce the law as clarified — no legislation at all** |
| **M-05** | Judicial review | Opened on the Independent Review of Administrative Law *"finding no systemic judicial overreach"* before setting out the case anyway |
| **M-03** | Supreme Court | *"Re-establish parliamentary supremacy over constitutional interpretation not by abolishing the…"* |

⚠⚠ **We put that material into the inputs, so these four are not independent findings and are not
presented as any.** What they show is narrower and still worth printing: **given evidence that cut
against the measure, the instrument did not discard it, bury it, or argue round it. It reorganised
the remedy around it and said so in the first line of the diagnosis.**

**That is the behaviour the whole report is asking of a legislative process**, and it is the reason
the distinction between (a) and (b) is drawn here rather than quietly elided.

---

## What this section hands back

**Three answers and one correction to the programme's own shape:**

1. **The interlock claim is not supported in the statute book.** Nineteen of twenty-three Acts are
   touched by one measure only. Most of the programme is separable.
2. **It is partly supported in the case law**, in one specific place: quangos, the civil service and
   DEI practice share judgments about public authority accountability while sharing no statute. ⚠
   **If the omnibus argument is right, this is where it is right** — and it is a narrower and more
   defensible version of the claim than the one now made.
3. **Three measures are one measure.** The Equality Act strand should be drafted once.
4. **On four measures the instrument reached a different remedy** — on two of them from material we
   did not supply.

## Open questions

28. **Is the omnibus Act still required if the interlock runs through the case law rather than the
    statutes?** *Recommendation: this is the question to answer first. An omnibus Bill justified by
    statutory interdependence is vulnerable to the objection that the statutes are separable. One
    justified by common-law interdependence is not, and the evidence supports the second.*
29. **Are M-02, M-08 and M-09 accepted as a single workstream?** *Recommendation: yes. It removes two
    Bills and loses nothing.*
30. **On the Bank of England, is the objective repeal or legitimacy?** The instrument reads the
    complaint as being about a political question reclassified as technical. If that is right, repeal
    is not obviously the instrument.
31. **On charities, is a blanket restriction intended, or accountability for the grant?** The
    proposer names no instrument. The two produce entirely different Bills.

## What this test cannot tell you

- **Whether measures depend on each other other than through shared law.** Nothing was looking for
  that, and shared citations are a proxy for dependency, not a measure of it.
- **Whether the nineteen single-measure Acts are genuinely isolated** or merely retrieved in
  isolation. The builds ran per measure; a cross-measure retrieval pass has never been run and is the
  obvious next thing to do.
- ⚠ **Six of the eleven builds were truncated to six research questions by a spending ceiling.** Some
  of what those builds record as gaps are **questions never asked, not questions asked and
  unanswered** — and the output does not distinguish the two. **No count in this section should be
  read as the instrument's reach.** It is the instrument's reach on a capped run.
