## Part B — the template your notes imply, generalised to all twelve

This is the structure I would apply to every measure. **It needs your sign-off before I rewrite twelve measures**, because it moves sections between parts of the document and changes what the banner does on every page.

### Each measure becomes two banner-headed blocks

**1 · THE STRATEGIC KERNEL** — right-aligned in the banner, with three coloured-bar headings running across the page:

| Heading | What goes in it | Where it comes from now |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis** | The problem as stated (1st para), then its causes, then why it persists | "The question examined" 1st para + "What is wrong, and why it persists" |
| **Guiding policy** | What David proposes, what he has ruled out, the approach | "The question examined" paras 2–3 + "The approach proposed" |
| **Coherent actions** | Each step and who does it, ending at the operational-actions table | "What would actually have to be done" |

**2 · DECISIONS, CHALLENGES AND RESEARCH** — everything outside the kernel:

| Section | Note |
|---|---|
| **Consequential decisions** | unchanged |
| **Challenges to the proposal** | "Each one is a question…" |
| **The critique revisions** | new section, with an intro paragraph explaining what the critique is |
| **Difficulties caused by the law today** | was "The law as it stands" |
| **What the courts have said** | unchanged |
| **Evidence that runs the other way** | unchanged |
| **The evidence base** | the retrieval tables move here, and the "understanding the terrain" line goes |

### Three rules applied to all twelve

1. **Say why it is a problem and what harm it does** — briefly, at the head of the diagnosis. For the HRA the Prosperity Institute report answers this; the other eleven need the equivalent.
2. **Say why it persists in terms of who defends it** — who benefits, who identifies with it, who will fight for it.
3. **Reframe the "claims and against" blocks as "The methods used by the Establishment to block change"** — each hurdle as a worked example of the current law being used to block action, and each one showing what repeal would answer.

### Two text rules applied everywhere

- **No "the research changed my mind" framing.** 24 of these across the two files. Each becomes: *the evidence says X; the conclusion is Y.* The revised conclusion is kept; the narrative of changing position goes.
- **"delegated" → "automated"** where it describes the mechanical amendments.

## Part C — three things only David can answer, raised by your notes

These are not edits. They change what the measure *is*, and the document must not decide them silently.

1. **Does the Equality Act measure keep the substantive anti-discrimination protections, or not?** The measure is currently written as "repeal s.149, keep the rest". Your note says you doubt that is David's position. **Until this is settled, 6.2 is written against the wrong proposal**, and so are its challenges and its consequential decisions. *Recommendation: settle this before Volume 2 goes out — it is the single largest open question in the volume.*
2. **If the substantive protections go too, does "protected characteristics" go with them** — replaced by a single instruction to appoint and procure on track record alone? That is a materially larger Bill than the one now drafted.
3. **Does the programme forbid devolved bodies and arm's-length bodies from positive discrimination**, or merely stop requiring it? A prohibition reaches Northern Ireland's section 75 and the devolution settlements; a repeal does not.
