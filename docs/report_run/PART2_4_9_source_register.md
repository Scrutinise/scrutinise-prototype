# Part 2 §4.9 — The published record: a triaged source register

**Draft 1, 2 Sep 2026, CCW.** Every row in this section carries a verification status. Nothing is
cited as established unless it has been checked against a primary or near-primary source, and the
URL is recorded.

---

## Why this section exists, and what it cost

Four large language models — Gemini, Grok, ChatGPT and Claude — were each given the same prompt,
separately, and asked to name the significant published commentary on the programme's four flagship
measures. They were deliberately **not** told what we already held, because a model shown our list
will confirm our list.

The exercise produced roughly **160 named sources**. It also produced a second result we did not
plan for, and it is the more important one.

⚠⚠ **Of the items named by only one model and then checked, a substantial number do not exist.**
Not misremembered — invented, complete with committee paper numbers, lecture series, named research
leads and plausible years. Five were checked in a single pass and **five of five were fabrications.**

This is set out at the front of the section rather than buried, for two reasons. First, because the
report's own credibility depends on the reader knowing how we treat unverified material. Second,
because **it is the clearest demonstration in this document of what Scrutinise is for.** A programme
drafted from AI-assisted research, without a verification step, will cite things that do not exist,
and will do so in a document put before Parliament.

## Part B — The three documented negatives

⚠⚠ **Three of the four models, searching independently, report the same absences.** Converging
negatives from independent searches are the strongest form this evidence takes, and they answer the
"what was tried before" question for three of the four flagship measures. The answer is: **nothing
has been tried.**

| Measure | The documented negative | Named by |
|---|---|---|
| **CRAG 2010 Part 1** | No Bill has ever been introduced to repeal Part 1. No government or independent review has recommended it. No substantial published legal case for it has been found. | Grok, ChatGPT, Claude — three of four, each explicitly |
| **Abolition of the Supreme Court** | No Bill in the last fifteen years to abolish the UK Supreme Court and return final appeal to the Appellate Committee of the House of Lords | Grok, ChatGPT, Claude |
| **Repeal of the Equality Act as a whole** | No government-commissioned review in fifteen years has recommended repealing the Act as a whole. Substantial work exists on s.149; almost none on whole-Act repeal | Grok, ChatGPT, Claude |

**What this means for the report, stated plainly.** On the Human Rights Act the programme is in
crowded company: reviews, a Bill, a division, party policy, two live Bills. On the other three it is,
on the published record, **substantially alone**. That is not an argument against the measures. It is
a statement about how much preparatory work exists to draw on — and the honest reading is that for
CRAG Part 1 the drafting would be starting from nothing.

⚠ **A negative of this kind can never be proved.** It is three independent searches finding nothing,
not a demonstration that nothing exists. It is stated as what it is.

An earlier draft of §4.8 described **Lord Maude's Independent Review of Governance and
Accountability in the Civil Service** as "directly on point" for the CRAG measure and dated it 2024.
On checking:

| **Direction** | Treated as supporting the diagnosis | **Expressly retains the permanent impartial civil service** |

The review states that it "proceeds on the basis that the UK's current system of a permanent and
politically impartial Civil Service will be maintained."

⚠⚠ **That is the hardest single document in this section for the CRAG measure**, and it is hard in

**The question that puts to the proposers is not "are you right?" It is: what does Maude know that
makes him stop where he stops, and is that reason good?** He is not a defender of the machine.

## Part D — Equality Act: the Northern Ireland correction, stated precisely

⚠⚠ **A hard correction, checked against the statute because a report making a correction must not
itself be correctable.**

**Section 217 of the Equality Act 2010** provides that the Act forms part of the law of England and
Wales; of Scotland apart from s.190 and Part 15; and that **three provisions only** also form part of
the law of Northern Ireland — s.82 (offshore work), s.105(3)–(4), and s.199 (abolition of the
presumption of advancement).

**Section 149 is not among them.** Equal opportunities and discrimination are transferred matters
under the Northern Ireland Act 1998.

> **The Public Sector Equality Duty has never applied in Northern Ireland.** A Westminster Bill
> repealing s.149 would change nothing there.

**The nearest Northern Ireland analogue is s.75 of the Northern Ireland Act 1998**, which gave
legislative form to an equality-mainstreaming commitment in the Belfast/Good Friday Agreement and
came into force on 1 January 2000. ⚠ **It is an analogue, not an equivalent**, and the differences
matter to anyone drafting:

- **s.75 is a two-limb duty with differently weighted limbs.** Limb one is "due regard to the need to
  promote equality of opportunity" across the listed grounds; limb two is the weaker "regard to the
  desirability of promoting good relations", and only across three grounds — religious belief,
  political opinion, racial group. **s.149 has a three-limb structure with a uniform "due regard"
  standard throughout.**
- **Enforcement is structurally different.** s.75(4) routes enforcement through Schedule 9 — equality
  schemes approved by the Equality Commission for Northern Ireland, with screening and equality
  impact assessments. That is administrative machinery. **The PSED has no equivalent scheme-approval
  regime and is enforced principally by judicial review.**
- The familiar "nine grounds" formulation is shorthand, not statutory. The section lists them across
  four lettered paragraphs with six bundled into the first.

**A second usable point falls out of the same section.** s.217(2) shows the Act is not uniform even
across Great Britain: s.190 and Part 15 do not extend to Scotland. **The Equality Act is already a
patchwork**, and a repeal Bill has to be drafted against the patchwork rather than against the Act's
title.

⚠ **This changes what the Equality Act rows in the register mean.** They currently read as UK-wide.
They are not. **If the objective is to remove public-sector equality duties across the United
Kingdom, s.75 of the Northern Ireland Act 1998 is a separate target and it is not on the twelve-point
list.** And because it derives from the Belfast/Good Friday Agreement, it is a materially harder
target than s.149 — which is a question for the proposers, not a conclusion of ours.

## Part E — Where the official Opposition actually stands, in her own words

Three models named a **Kemi Badenoch speech at the Institute for Government on 9 June 2026**. It is
verified, and she draws the distinction the register needs **herself, in terms**:

> "That's why I can announce today that a Conservative government will repeal the Public Sector
> Equality Duty in its entirety. We do not need to replace the duty."

> "Reform… announced that they would abolish the entire Equality Act. So let me be clear, that would
> mean that it would be legal to discriminate against white people."

> "What Reform don't understand is that anti-discrimination legislation protects us all. Because it
> is race and sex that are protected, not being black or white, male or female. The Equality Act
> protects a white man as much as a black woman. But the Public Sector Equality Duty does not.
> That's why we are going to overhaul the Act, starting by removing this Duty."

⚠⚠ **That places three positions on one line, and the thesis is on the far end of it:**

| Position | Whose | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| Repeal s.149 and its machinery; keep the substantive protections | **Conservative** (Badenoch, June 2026) | Morrissey Bill 4189 |
| Abolish the entire Equality Act | **Reform UK** | No Bill located |
| Repeal the Equality Act | **The thesis, as we read it** | — |

**The thesis's position is nearer Reform's than the Conservatives'**, and the Leader of the
Opposition has publicly argued against it by name. **That is a fact about the political landscape the
programme will be drafted into, not a view of ours about which is right**, and it belongs in front of
the proposers before the Bill is drafted rather than after.

**The direct rebuttal is also on the record and is the strongest available:** Institute for
Government, Stuart Hoddinott and Cassia Rowland, *"Scrapping the Public Sector Equality Duty won't
create the change Badenoch wants"*, 11 June 2026 — two days later. It argues the duty does not
require equality of outcomes or positive action; that frontline staff do not cite it as a barrier;
and that removal is a misdiagnosis. ✔ Verified.

## Part G — Where the courts have already moved, verified

**For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers [2025] UKSC 16**, judgment 16 April 2025.

✔ **Citation confirmed. One model gave it as [2024] UKSC 12 — that is the Supreme Court *case
number* (UKSC 2024/0042), not the citation.** Our own draft was right; recorded because the near-miss
is instructive about how these errors are generated.

The Court held that "sex", "man" and "woman" in the Equality Act 2010 refer to biological sex, and
that a person with a Gender Recognition Certificate in the female gender does not come within the
Act's definition of "woman".

⚠ **A trap for anyone drafting from secondary summaries, recorded because we nearly fell into it.**
The Inner House below held the opposite way. An automated read of the case page returned the decision
*under appeal* as though it were the holding. **The Supreme Court reversed.** Anyone checking this
against a summary rather than the judgment can get the answer exactly backwards.

**What it does to register row SP-10** is set out in §4.8 and is unchanged: the position the thesis
objects to has been substantially resolved by litigation, in the direction the thesis wants, without
legislation.

## Part H — The reform-not-exit position, verified

**The Chișinău Declaration, CM(2026)99-final**, adopted at the Council of Europe's 135th Ministerial
Session in Chișinău on **15 May 2026**, by **all 46 member states**, as a political and non-binding
declaration. ✔ Verified.

⚠ **Correction to how it was described to us.** It is **not** a general declaration on the
interpretation of the Convention. It is specifically **on the ECHR and migration** — subsidiarity and
the margin of appreciation, Articles 3 and 8 in expulsion and extradition cases, mass arrivals, and
the instrumentalisation of migration.

**Why it belongs in the report even so.** A load-bearing premise of the thesis is that the Convention
system cannot be reformed and therefore only exit works. **The narrower reading of the Declaration
still contradicts that premise on the specific ground where the pressure is greatest** — Articles 3
and 8 in expulsion cases — because 46 states have just done, by political declaration, the thing the
premise says cannot be done.

⚠ **It also cuts the other way, and both halves should print.** A non-binding political declaration
is not an amendment to the Convention and does not bind the Strasbourg court. **Whether it changes
outcomes is exactly the empirical question**, and it is unresolved. It should be set out as a
contested development, not as a refutation.

## Part I — Sumption, resolved and unresolved

An open question in §4.6 was whether Lord Sumption endorses **withdrawal from the Convention**, as
distinct from repeal of the Human Rights Act. It is now resolved.

✔ **He endorses withdrawal.** *Daily Telegraph*, **6 November 2025**:

> "The time has come to bring rights back home by withdrawing from the convention and re-enacting it
> in an ordinary domestic statute."

⚠ *The headline is reported as "Bring rights back home to Britain: leave ECHR". One model gave a
different headline for the same piece. The quoted sentence is verified; the headline as we first
received it is not, and is not used.*

**This confirms §4.6's central finding rather than disturbing it.** Sumption's remedy is withdrawal
**plus domestic re-enactment of the same rights**. Wolfson warns against exactly that. The two most
senior legal voices on the same side of the argument still recommend opposite replacements.

### ✗ And one citation removed

A source described as **"Lord Sumption, *In defence of the UK Supreme Court*, Telegraph, 4 April
2025"** — arguing that the 2005 reform was largely cosmetic and that critics are misled by the
American name — was named by one model only. **It could not be verified.** A matching title string
appears in one search index on a syndication partner; the page could not be retrieved, no publisher
page was located, and no other record of Sumption defending the Court against abolition was found.

⚠ **It is not used.** It is recorded here because **if it is real it is highly significant** — it
would place Sumption against the thesis on the Supreme Court measure while with it on the ECHR — and
because a reader with a Telegraph archive can settle it in a minute. **That is a request, not a
finding.**

**Two observations that generalise.**

1. **Every fabrication had a real neighbour.** A real committee, a real lecture series, a real
   author, a real adjacent report — with one element changed. That is what makes them survive a
   casual read: nothing is absurd, and the correct citation is often one search away.
2. **The three models that flagged their own uncertainty produced almost none of these.** Two models
   volunteered explicit "what I could not confirm" sections and were right to. The model that
   produced all five fabrications flagged nothing. ⚠ **Confidence in the output was inversely
   related to its reliability**, which is exactly the failure mode a legislative drafting process
   cannot tolerate.

**What we did with them.** Nothing enters this report on a single unverified model citation. Where a
fabrication had a real neighbour, the neighbour is now on the reading list — Yowell 2021 in
particular, which is the substantial think-tank treatment of s.149 the invented Policy Exchange
report was standing in for.

### The framing question the models converged on

⚠ **All four models, unprompted, reframed the question the same way** — from "common law versus
foreign law" to **political constitutionalism versus legal constitutionalism**, tracing to J. A. G.
Griffith's 1979 Chorley Lecture *The Political Constitution*.

**Why that reframing helps rather than hinders the programme.** On the first framing, the thesis is a
British-versus-European argument, and Sir John Laws's common-law constitutionalism is fatal to it. On
the second, the thesis joins a debate with serious voices on both sides, in which **Griffith,
Tomkins, Loughlin and Bellamy make the same diagnosis from the left** — that political choices have
migrated to legal and expert bodies — while disagreeing about the remedy.

⚠ **That is the strongest available repositioning of the thesis, and it is not ours: it is what four
independent models said the literature is about.** It is offered to the proposers as an option, not
urged.

## What this section establishes

1. **Two of the twelve measures are already before Parliament in narrower form**, and the narrowing
   is the thing to explain.
2. **Three of the twelve have no precedent at all** — no Bill, no review, no developed legal case.
3. **The Equality Act measure does not do what the list assumes it does** in Northern Ireland.
4. **The most senior sympathetic review of the civil service stops short of the remedy**, and says
   why.
5. **AI-assisted research produced confident fabrications at a material rate.** Verification is not
   an optional finishing step. It is the product.
