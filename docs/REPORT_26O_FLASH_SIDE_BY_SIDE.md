**Working title.** Establishing Personal Operational Accountability in the Civil Service

**The problem.** Public administration across government departments and arm's-length bodies routinely suffers from chronic delay and inaction because decision-making responsibility is deliberately atomised across layers of officials and legal advisers who face no personal consequence for failure or obstruction.

**Who is affected.** cost: Direct financial waste from duplicated administrative salaries and stalled sector investment (such as repairs to care facilities); the aggregate system-wide cost is not established. · impact: Years of paralysis and protracted refusal to make decisions or engage, causing direct operational hardship to regulated sectors and stalling public policy implementation. · evidence: First-hand testimony from the proposer describing a 4-year administrative delay on a Northern Ireland care home VAT structure where government lawyers refused to meet, despite identical private legal work being completed in an afternoon; documented failures of Whitehall accountability in Public Administration Committee inquiries. · affectedGroups: Citizens and enterprises requiring administrative, tax, or regulatory determinations from government bodies (specifically highlighted: care home operators in Northern Ireland seeking VAT clarification), as well as taxpayers and elected ministers.

**Root cause.** The constitutional doctrine of ministerial responsibility, entrenched by the Carltona principle, legally collapses departmental action into the person of the Minister, relieving individual civil servants of direct operational liability and incentivising the diffusion of responsibility across internal hierarchies.

**Pivotal obstacle.** The persistent institutional lock-in where senior civil service leadership and Whitehall departmental hierarchies actively benefit from the Carltona shield and Osmotherly convention, successfully resisting reform by warning that piercing official anonymity undermines civil service neutrality, while relying on the statutory absence of delivery-based dismissal frameworks in CRAG 2010 to dilute internal managerial accountability into non-enforceable process.

**Summary of Diagnosis.** Civil service administrative stagnation and delay persist because the constitutional doctrine of ministerial responsibility, entrenched by the Carltona principle, legally collapses departmental action into the Minister, relieving individual civil servants of direct operational liability and incentivising the diffusion of responsibility across internal hierarchies. As demonstrated by a 4-year impasse over a care home VAT structure in Northern Ireland where officials and government legal advisers diffused decisions and refused technical dialogue, officials incur professional risk by taking individual operational initiative while facing zero personal consequence for obfuscation or collective inaction. Because Part 1 of the Constitutional Reform and Governance Act 2010 (CRAG) focuses on merit-based appointment and codes rather than delivery capability, and explicitly excludes the Northern Ireland Civil Service under s.1(2)(d), neither Whitehall nor devolved administrations possess an enforceable statutory feedback loop connecting personal performance to operational consequence.

**Guiding Policy.** Displace the Carltona presumption for major administrative determinations by establishing an explicit statutory 'Senior Operational Owner' regime with mandatory timeline limits, using executive powers under CRAG 2010 s.3 to tie senior civil service performance management directly to audited delivery milestones.

**What it rules out.** Rules out relying on internal, non-binding civil service managerial charters, voluntary capability reviews, or general transparency dashboards that lack personal termination or demotion consequences. Rules out universal Carltona repeal across all core policy-advising functions, preserving ministerial agency for broad political discretion while carving out operational and regulatory determinations. Rules out relying on Westminster CRAG 2010 amendments alone to resolve devolved administrative blockages in Northern Ireland.

**Leverage.** This approach exploits the asymmetry of operational ownership: inertia currently flourishes because diffusing a decision across multiple departments carries zero personal cost, whereas concentrating legal delivery responsibility into a single named Senior Responsible Owner makes hiding within committee consensus impossible.

**Conditions for success.** • Statements of Responsibility must be legally registered with explicit, exclusive operational owners for all major administrative workflows and statutory approvals, leaving zero unassigned residual duties.

**Root cause.** The prevailing culture within the civil service incentivises the diffusion of responsibility and risk aversion.

**Pivotal obstacle.** The absence of any mechanism to enforce *individual* accountability for performance. The doctrine of Ministerial Responsibility makes the Minister accountable for everything, which in practice means no single official is accountable for anything. This structural shield allows the culture of risk-aversion and responsibility-diffusion to persist, as there are no personal consequences for delay and failure, and no rewards for timely delivery.

**Summary of Diagnosis.** The core problem is a civil service culture that diffuses responsibility, a rational response to a system where blame for failure is career-ending but reward for success is absent. As the proposer experienced, this leads to institutional paralysis, where officials avoid individual ownership, taking four years for a task a private lawyer did in an afternoon. This culture persists because there is no effective mechanism for enforcing individual accountability; the constitutional doctrine of Ministerial Responsibility shields officials, while internal performance management lacks meaningful consequences.

**Guiding Policy.** Implement a robust, transparent performance management framework for individual civil servants, linking specific outcomes to personal accountability and career progression.

**What it rules out.** This policy rules out treating accountability as a collective responsibility. It rejects reforms that focus only on departmental-level targets, purely internal HR processes that lack transparency, or relying on the existing doctrine of Ministerial Responsibility to drive operational performance.

**Leverage.** This approach directly targets the incentive structure within the civil service, shifting it from risk aversion and responsibility diffusion to one that rewards individual ownership and measurable delivery, thereby changing the underlying culture that perpetuates inefficiency and lack of accountability.

**Conditions for success.** • Clear, measurable, and relevant performance metrics are developed for a wide range of civil service roles, including those in policy development and advisory functions, and these metrics are regularly reviewed for effectiveness.

• A clear, transparent, and timely appeals process is established for civil servants who believe their performance evaluations are unfair or inaccurate.
• The system explicitly differentiates between systemic failures requiring process changes and individual performance failures, as the proposer suggests, to ensure appropriate responses.

**Summary of Guiding Policy.** Shift accountability from the abstract (the 'Department' or the Minister) to the specific (a named, responsible individual) for every significant decision and project. This will be achieved by making individual performance and its consequences—positive and negative—personal, public, and unavoidable.

**Summary of Coherent Actions.** First, use existing powers under the Civil Service Management Code to mandate that a single, named Senior Responsible Owner (SRO) is assigned to every major project and policy, with their name and responsibilities published. Second, link the performance evaluation of these SROs directly to the delivery of these published outcomes, making this the primary factor in their promotion and pay. Third, create an independent oversight body to audit and publicly report on the effectiveness of this new accountability framework, ensuring it is applied consistently and not gamed.

**Causes.**
- (material) The prevailing culture within the civil service incentivises the diffusion of responsibility and risk aversion.
- (material) Lack of clear, measurable performance metrics and individual responsibility for outcomes within the civil service.
- (contributory) The doctrine of Ministerial Responsibility, while constitutionally sound, inadvertently diffuses individual civil servant accountability.

**Coherent actions.**

This framework should explicitly define individual responsibilities within projects and operational tasks. — Cabinet Office, in consultation with individual departments

This system must differentiate between systemic failures and individual performance issues. — Civil Service HR, Departmental Line Managers

Emphasize the importance of individual ownership and decision-making. — Civil Service Learning, Departmental Learning & Development teams
