## §2 — Written evidence to a select committee

⚠ **This is the output that reaches Parliament without needing an MP.** Anyone may submit, it is
published, and it enters the record.

**2a. Derive the shape from the corpus, not from guidance you recall.** ⚠ **The platform holds about
140,567 sections of committee evidence** — thousands of real examples of the genre, with 97.7% of
written evidence carrying an attribution. **Read a sample and report what a submission actually looks
like**: length, numbering, whether a summary leads, how interests are declared, how sources are cited.

**2b.** ⚠ **Then check the current published guidance** — committees state their own requirements and
they change. **Report where the corpus sample and the current guidance disagree**, and follow the
guidance.

**2c.** ⚠⚠ **Written evidence is submitted to a specific inquiry and addresses its terms of reference.
Generic evidence is not a thing.** **Report whether we can identify open inquiries at all** — the
corpus holds published evidence, which is by definition past inquiries. **If we cannot, say so**, and
the template produces a submission the user adapts, with that limitation stated on it.

**2d. What it contains**, subject to 2a: who is submitting and any declarable interest · a short
summary · the problem and its diagnosis · the proposed approach · what it would take · the principal
objections and the response to them · what remains unresolved · sources.

**2e.** ⚠ **The honest rows survive.** A submission that omits *what nobody has measured* is a worse
submission, not a tidier one.

## §3 — The one-page summary

**For someone with two minutes**: a minister, an MP between meetings, a journalist.

**3a. One page. A hard limit, enforced, not a target.** ⚠ **Report what happens when the content does
not fit** — truncation is not acceptable, and neither is two pages. **Propose how it decides what to
drop.**

**3b. What it carries:** the problem in a sentence · what would be different · the approach · the
instrument it would take · the strongest argument for · **the strongest argument against** · what is
still unresolved · where the evidence comes from.

**3c.** ⚠⚠ **This is the document most likely to be forwarded alone, detached from everything that
qualifies it.** So **every caveat it needs is on the page itself, never by reference.** The same rule
as a claim and its grounds: what makes it honest cannot be separable from what makes it persuasive.

**3d.** ⚠ **The strongest argument against is not optional and is not softened.** A one-pager that
gives only the case for is advocacy, and this platform is not that. **It comes from the challenges,
which are the sharpest content the build produces.**
